---
origin: Vietnam · Creation myth of the Việt people, from ancient oral tradition
---

## legend

In the beginning there was no world. There were no mountains and no rivers, no animals and no people. Heaven and earth were pressed together into one dark, cold, shapeless mass, and nothing could live between them.

Then, out of that darkness, a giant god appeared. He was so huge that no one could say how long his legs were. The old tellers call him Thần Trụ Trời, the god who pillared the sky.

The giant stood up, and as he rose he pushed the sky upward with his head. But he could not stand holding it up forever. So he began to dig. He scooped up earth with his great hands, hauled up rocks, and heaped them into a pillar, thick and tall, to prop up the sky. The higher the pillar grew, the higher the sky rose. He dug and heaped, dug and heaped, and the pillar pushed the sky up and up until it reached the blue clouds.

From that day heaven and earth were two. The sky curved over the world like a great upturned bowl, and the earth lay flat and broad beneath it. Light could come in at last.

When the sky was high and had set firm, the giant no longer needed his pillar. He smashed it apart and flung its earth and stones in every direction. Wherever a stone landed it became a mountain or an island, and wherever the earth fell it became a hill or a mound. That is why the ground is no longer flat as it was in the beginning. And the deep places where he had dug out earth and rock for his pillar filled with water and became the wide sea.

Thần Trụ Trời was only the first of the giant gods. After him came others to finish shaping the world, and people still chant their names in an old folk rhyme: the one who counts the sand, the one who bails the sea, the one who counts the stars, the one who digs the rivers, the one who plants the trees, the one who raises the hills, and the one who pillars the sky.

## beats

- chaos: Heaven and earth churn as one dark mass, and the giant sleeps curled in the thin seam of light between them.
- wake: The giant wakes and rises to his knees, his broad back pushing up against the sky.
- dig: Holding the sky on his shoulders, he scoops up earth and rock and heaps them into a rough pillar.
- pillar: Block by block the pillar grows, and the sky above lifts and curves into a great dome.
- light: Heaven and earth come apart for the first time, dawn light floods in and the first stars appear.
- break: With the sky set high, he smashes the pillar and its rocks and clay fly in every direction.
- mountains: The flying stones land as mountains, hills and a chain of small islands, each in a puff of dust.
- waters: The pits he dug fill with water, springs burst out, and a river winds down to a newborn sea.
- first day: The first sun rises between the new mountains, green spreads over the hills and Lạc birds wheel past as the giant rests.
- return: Dusk and mist fall, the sky sinks back onto the earth, and the giant curls up to sleep so the tale can begin again.
