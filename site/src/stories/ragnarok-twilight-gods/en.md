## legend

The world of the Norse gods hangs in the branches of a single ash tree, Yggdrasil, whose roots reach down to the realms of the dead and whose crown holds up the sky. At its foot the watchman Heimdall guards Bifröst, the rainbow bridge between the home of the gods and the world of men. He needs less sleep than a bird, and can hear the grass grow. And he waits, because the gods know how their story ends.

First comes the Fimbulwinter: three winters with no summer between them, snow blowing from every side, brothers turning against brothers. Then the two wolves that have chased the sun and the moon since the beginning of time finally catch them and swallow them, and the sky goes dark.

Heimdall lifts the Gjallarhorn and blows, and the sound rolls out across all nine worlds. The earth shakes. Every chain breaks. The giant wolf Fenrir, bound by the gods with a ribbon soft as silk and stronger than any iron, tears himself free, his lower jaw on the earth and his upper jaw against the sky. The World Serpent, Jörmungandr, heaves its coils out of the sea, flooding the land and spitting venom into the air.

Thor, the thunderer, faces the serpent. He strikes it again and again with his hammer Mjölnir and at last kills it. But the venom has touched him, and he walks nine steps and falls dead. Odin is swallowed by the wolf. From the south comes Surtr, the fire giant, with a sword brighter than the sun, and when the armies of fire ride over Bifröst, the bridge breaks beneath them. Surtr flings fire across the world, flames climb Yggdrasil to its crown, and the burning earth sinks into the sea. The stars fall from the sky.

And then there is silence, and dark water.

But that is not the end. Out of the sea a new earth rises, green and fresh, with waterfalls pouring from its cliffs and an eagle hunting fish below them. Two humans, Líf and Lífþrasir, who hid in a wood through the fire, step out into the light. A new sun, daughter of the old, climbs the sky. And in the grass the young gods find the golden game pieces their fathers once played with, as if the world were only setting up its board again.

## beats

- yggdrasil: Golden dusk; the world tree sways, two ravens circle its crown, and Heimdall stands guard.
- winter: The Fimbulwinter falls, the sea ices over, and the wolves chase down the sun and the moon.
- horn: Heimdall blows the Gjallarhorn and rings of sound roll across the ice.
- unchained: Fenrir snaps the silk ribbon Gleipnir, his jaws gaping from sea to sky.
- serpent: Jörmungandr heaves out of the sea, spraying venom over the rocks.
- thunder: Thor hurls Mjölnir until the serpent falls, then staggers nine steps and falls too.
- fire: Surtr rises beyond the fjord, sweeps his blazing sword, and Bifröst shatters.
- burning: Flames race up Yggdrasil to its crown under a red sky.
- sinking: The burning land sinks into the sea as the stars fall.
- silence: Only dark, calm water and a few slow stars.
- rising: A new green land rises from the sea, and an eagle dives for a fish.
- return: The world tree and the rainbow bridge grow back, two survivors step out, and golden game pieces glint in the grass.
