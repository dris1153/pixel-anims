## legend

Long ago, the people of the Andes lived like wild animals. They had no villages and no fields, dressed in skins and leaves, and ate whatever roots and berries they could find. Inti, the Sun, looked down on them from the sky and took pity on them.

So the Sun sent two of his children to earth: his son Manco Cápac and his daughter Mama Ocllo, who were also husband and wife. He set them down in Lake Titicaca, high among the snowy mountains, and they rose out of its shining waters. Before they went, the Sun gave Manco a staff of gold and told him: "Go wherever you wish. Wherever you stop to eat or sleep, push this staff into the ground. Where it sinks in at a single thrust, there you shall build your city, and teach the people to live as human beings."

The two walked north through the mountains. At every place they rested, Manco pushed the golden staff into the earth, and every time it stopped short on hard ground. They crossed high passes and cold plains where llamas grazed, and still the staff would not sink.

At last they came down into a green valley ringed by mountains. Manco pressed the staff into the soil, and it slid in easily, down and down, until it vanished completely. This was the place the Sun had chosen. There they founded Cusco, whose name, the old chroniclers said, means the navel of the world.

Then Manco Cápac and Mama Ocllo went out into the hills and gathered the scattered people, who followed them in wonder at their fine clothes and wise words. Manco taught the men to plough, to plant maize and potatoes, to dig canals and to build houses of stone. Mama Ocllo taught the women to spin wool and weave fine cloth. Cusco grew into a city, and at its heart rose a temple to the Sun, their father. From these two came the line of Inca kings, who would one day rule an empire stretching along the Andes.

Every year, at the winter solstice in June, the Incas held Inti Raymi, the great Festival of the Sun, with offerings, music and dancing. In Cusco it is still celebrated today.

## beats

- titicaca: At dawn Inti rises from behind the Island of the Sun and spreads his rays across Lake Titicaca.
- emerge: Manco Cápac and Mama Ocllo rise from the sparkling foam in bright woven cloth and gold ear discs.
- journey: They walk over the mountains past grazing llamas, testing the golden staff, but it will not sink.
- sink: In the green valley the staff sinks to its tip in a flash of gold, and the land glows.
- cusco: Fitted stone walls with trapezoid doors rise, and the golden Temple of the Sun gleams at the centre.
- teach: Terraces of maize and potatoes climb the hills as Manco teaches farming and Mama Ocllo weaves striped cloth.
- raymi: At Inti Raymi dancers in red and gold spin, a great sun disc flashes and a condor soars overhead.
- night: The Milky Way wheels over the quiet city, which fades into the mist as dawn breaks over the lake again.
