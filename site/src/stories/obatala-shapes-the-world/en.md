---
origin: Nigeria · Yoruba creation myth of Ile-Ife, from oral tradition
---

## legend

In the beginning there was only the sky above and the water below. In the sky lived Olorun, the owner of the heavens, whom the Yoruba also call Olodumare, together with the orishas, the gods. Below lay nothing but marsh and misty sea, the realm of Olokun, goddess of the deep waters. Nothing grew there, and no one lived there.

Obatala, the orisha who dresses all in white, looked down at the empty water and felt sorry for it. He asked Olorun to let him make solid land, and Olorun agreed. So Obatala went to Orunmila, the wise diviner, to learn what he would need. "A chain of gold long enough to reach the water," said Orunmila, "a snail shell full of sand, a hen, a black cat and a palm nut." The other orishas gave him all the gold they had, and the goldsmith forged it into a chain.

Obatala hooked the chain to the edge of the sky and climbed down, link by link, until he reached the very end. It still hung above the water. As Orunmila had told him, he poured the sand from the snail shell, and a little mound rose from the sea. Then he let the hen go. She scratched and kicked in every direction, and wherever the sand flew, land spread out into hills and valleys. Obatala stepped down onto it. The place where he landed became Ile-Ife, the holy city where the Yoruba say the world began.

He planted the palm nut, and a palm tree grew, and then a whole forest. For a while Obatala lived there happily with his black cat. But he grew lonely, and began shaping little figures out of clay. In the heat he drank palm wine as he worked, and some of the figures came out crooked. Then Olorun breathed on them, and they woke and became the first people. When Obatala was sober again and saw the crooked ones, he swore never to drink again, and ever since he has watched over those who are born different.

The people built houses, and Ile-Ife grew into a busy town. But Olokun was furious, because no one had asked her before taking her waters. She sent great waves crashing over the land, drowning the fields and houses. The people cried out to the sky, and Orunmila came down the chain and drove the waters back, so the land was safe once more.

## beats

- waters: Violet dawn over the endless marsh, where only the glints of the sea goddess Olokun move beneath the mist.
- chain: Obatala, robed in white, lowers the golden chain link by link from the shining realm in the clouds.
- descend: He climbs down the swaying chain carrying the snail shell, a five-toed hen and the palm nut.
- sand: Hanging just above the water, he pours sand from the snail shell and a small golden mound rises.
- hen: The hen hops down and scratches, flinging sand everywhere, and hills and valleys spread across the water.
- palm: The palm nut shoots up into a tall palm, then a forest of palms and kola trees full of birds.
- clay: Beside a pond Obatala shapes rows of little clay people and sets them to dry in the sun.
- breath: Olodumare breathes a swirl of light over the figures, and they stand, stretch and dance to talking drums.
- ife: Ile-Ife rises with red earth walls, thatched roofs, a market of indigo and orange cloth, drummers and a palace.
- olokun: At dusk Olokun's jealous waves wash over the land and the city sinks, and the chain slides back up, ready to fall again.
