## legend

Amaterasu, the sun goddess, ruled the High Plain of Heaven, Takamagahara. Her light shone over heaven and earth, she tended the rice fields of heaven, and in her sacred weaving hall the robes of the gods were woven. But her brother Susanoo, god of storms, would not be tamed.

He broke down the ridges between her rice fields and filled in the ditches, and he defiled the hall where she held the feast of the first fruits. At first she excused him. Then, while she sat in the weaving hall, he tore a hole in its roof and flung down a piebald horse he had flayed. A weaving maiden, in her fright, was struck by her shuttle and died.

Amaterasu was terrified. She went into the Heavenly Rock Cave, Ama-no-Iwato, and shut its stone door behind her. At once heaven and earth fell into darkness, and the night never ended. Evil spirits swarmed, buzzing like summer flies, and every kind of misfortune arose.

So the eight hundred myriad gods gathered on the dry bed of the heavenly river, Ama-no-Yasukawa, and wise Omoikane thought of a plan. First they made the long-crowing birds crow. Ishikoridome made the great Yata mirror, and Tamanoya strung a long cord of curved magatama jewels, later two of the three sacred treasures of Japan. The gods uprooted a sakaki tree from heavenly Mount Kagu and hung the jewels in its upper branches, the mirror in the middle and white and blue cloth below. Futodama held it up as an offering, Ame-no-Koyane recited solemn prayers, and mighty Tajikarao hid beside the door.

Then Ame-no-Uzume turned a tub upside down before the cave and danced on it, stamping until it boomed. She danced as if possessed, so wildly that her robes came loose, and the High Plain of Heaven shook as all the gods roared with laughter.

Inside, Amaterasu wondered. She opened the door a crack and asked, "With me hidden, heaven and earth should be dark. Why does Uzume dance, and why do all the gods laugh?" "We rejoice," said Uzume, "because a deity nobler than you is here." As she spoke, Ame-no-Koyane and Futodama held up the mirror. Amaterasu saw her own shining face and, more and more amazed, stepped a little way out. At once Tajikarao took her hand and drew her out, and Futodama stretched a sacred shimenawa rope behind her. "You may go back no further than this," he said. And light returned to heaven and earth.

As for Susanoo, the gods punished him and cast him down from heaven to earth, where his next tale, with the eight-headed serpent Yamata no Orochi, begins.

## beats

- heaven: Golden light fills heaven as Amaterasu, haloed with sunrays, weaves at her loom beside the shining green rice fields of heaven.
- storm: Susanoo storms through in a gale, smashing the rice-field ridges and hurling a piebald horse through the weaving hall roof.
- hide: Amaterasu flees into the Heavenly Rock Cave, the great stone door grinds shut behind her, and her light goes out.
- dark: Darkness falls over heaven and earth, and evil spirits swarm through it, buzzing like summer flies.
- gather: By torchlight the eight hundred myriad gods crowd the riverbed of Ama-no-Yasukawa while wise Omoikane ponders a plan.
- birds: The long-crowing birds are set on a perch and crow all together into the endless night.
- mirror: A great sakaki tree rises before the cave, hung with the Yata mirror, strings of magatama jewels and white cloth streamers.
- dance: Ame-no-Uzume leaps onto an overturned tub and dances wildly, stamping, her sleeves flying and sakaki leaves whirling around her.
- laugh: The gods roar with laughter until heaven shakes and the torch flames leap high.
- peek: The door opens a crack, a blade of light cuts the dark, and the raised mirror flashes back her own shining face.
- open: Tajikarao flings the door away (a later legend says it became Mount Togakushi), light floods out with Amaterasu, and a shimenawa bars her way back.
- return: Clouds sweep across heaven, and when they pass the door is back in place and Amaterasu weaves again in the golden sun.
