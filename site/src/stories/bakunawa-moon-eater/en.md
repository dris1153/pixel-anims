origin: Philippines · Visayan folklore

## legend

When the world was young, the god Bathala hung seven moons in the night sky, so that the dark would never be too dark. Their light lay on the sea like seven silver roads, and the fishermen of the islands could find their way home at any hour.

But deep under the sea lived the Bakunawa, a serpent as long as a coastline, with fins like sails, red gills and a mouth as wide as a lake. From the bottom of the ocean it looked up at the moons and loved them, the way a hungry thing loves what it wants to eat. One night it rose from the waves, opened its jaws and swallowed a moon whole. The next night it swallowed another. One by one it ate them, until only a single moon was left in all the sky.

The people saw that soon the nights would be black for ever. So when the Bakunawa rose again, and the last moon began to darken and turn red inside its throat, they ran out of their houses with everything that could make a noise. They beat gongs and pots and pans, they shouted and sang and stamped, until the whole island rang.

The Bakunawa hated the noise. It shook its great head, it writhed, and at last it spat the moon back out into the sky and sank beneath the sea, sulking.

It still comes back. Whenever the moon grows dark and red in an eclipse, it is the Bakunawa trying once more to swallow it. And in the old days, people on the islands would still run out with their gongs and pots to make it let go, and they would dance on the beach when the moon came back whole.

Some say that on very clear nights, if you look carefully, you can still see the six lost moons, faint as ghosts, shining from inside the serpent's belly.

## beats

- moon: The full moon shines over the village, and faint ghosts of the six lost moons shimmer beside it.
- rise: The sea bulges and the Bakunawa bursts up in a spray of foam.
- swallow: It snaps its jaws around the moon; sky and sea bleed red as the moon slides down its throat.
- gongs: The villagers pour out banging gongs and pots, and rings of sound roll up at the serpent.
- spit: The glowing lump rises back up its throat and the Bakunawa spits the moon back into the sky.
- dive: The serpent sinks beneath the waves with a great splash.
- festival: The villagers dance with torches and sky lanterns drift up into the night.
- calm: Everyone goes home, the sea goes still, and the ghost moons shimmer again.
