origin: Mongolia · Folktale of the first morin khuur, the horse-head fiddle

## legend

Long ago, on the wide grasslands of Mongolia, there lived a poor shepherd boy named Suho. He had only his old grandmother, a small flock of sheep and a white felt ger. Every evening he sang as he brought the sheep home, and his voice carried far across the steppe.

One evening Suho did not come home at the usual hour, and his grandmother began to worry. At last he walked out of the dusk with something small and white in his arms. It was a newborn foal. "I found it alone in the grass," he said. "There was no mother anywhere. If I had left it, the wolves would have had it by morning."

Suho cared for the foal day and night, and it grew into a horse as white as snow, so strong and beautiful that everyone admired it. One night the sheep cried out in fear. Suho ran outside and found a wolf crouched by the flock, and his white horse standing between them, holding it off. The horse was soaked with sweat. Suho stroked its neck and said, "Thank you. From now on we will always be together."

One spring the lord of the land announced a horse race: whoever won would marry his daughter. Suho rode the white horse, and it flew far ahead of all the others. But when the lord saw that the winner was only a poor shepherd, he broke his word. He tossed Suho three silver coins and told him to leave the horse and go. Suho refused to sell his friend, so the lord's men beat him and took the horse away.

The lord held a feast to show off his prize. But the moment he climbed on, the white horse reared, threw him to the ground and galloped off. "Shoot it!" the lord shouted. Arrows flew and many struck home, yet the horse ran on and on until it reached Suho's ger. There it lay down beside him, and soon after it died.

Suho grieved and could not sleep. Then one night the white horse came to him in a dream. "Don't be sad," it said. "Make an instrument from my bones, my hide, my sinews and my hair. Then I will always be with you."

So Suho made a fiddle and carved a horse's head at the top of its neck. Whenever he played it, he remembered galloping across the grass on his white horse, and the music spread over the steppe and comforted all who heard it. That was the first morin khuur, the horse-head fiddle of Mongolia.

## beats

- foal: At dusk the shepherd boy Suho finds a tiny white foal alone in the grass and carries it home.
- grow: The foal grows into a shining white horse, and the two gallop across the steppe as the sheep scatter.
- wolf: At night a wolf creeps toward the flock, and the white horse rears and drives it away.
- naadam: At the Naadam race, among flags and cheering crowds, the white horse wins far ahead of the rest.
- taken: The rich lord in his fur hat seizes the horse and pushes Suho away.
- escape: The horse throws the lord, runs home through a rain of arrows, lies down by Suho's ger and fades into light.
- dream: The horse appears to Suho in a glowing dream and tells him to make an instrument from its hair and bones.
- fiddle: Suho plays the first horse-head fiddle by the fire, its music becomes spirit horses galloping across the stars, and dawn breaks.
