## legend

In Chinese, the word for an immortal, xian, is written with two small signs side by side: a person and a mountain. For more than two thousand years, people told of hermits who climbed into the high peaks, far above the dust of the world, to become just that. The old sage Zhuangzi wrote of a holy being on a distant mountain who ate no grain, breathed the wind, drank the dew and rode the clouds. Seekers followed, practising breathing and meditation and brewing elixirs in bronze cauldrons, hoping to grow so light and pure that they would never die.

Daoist teachers spoke of qi, the life force that flows through heaven, earth and the human body. By guiding it with the breath and gathering it in the dantian, a field of energy below the navel, a patient student could slowly refine body and spirit. Those who went furthest, the stories said, could fly on the clouds and live for ages.

Many immortals carried a sword. Lü Dongbin, one of the famous Eight Immortals, is always painted with a sword on his back, a blade to cut through demons and worldly desire. Tales from the Tang dynasty told of wandering swordsmen and swordswomen with uncanny powers, such as Nie Yinniang, who was trained by a mysterious nun until she could strike and vanish like a shadow. Over the centuries, storytellers let the swords themselves do more and more: slip from their sheaths, fly off to strike an enemy far away and return in a flash of light.

In the twentieth century, novels such as Legend of the Swordsmen of the Mountains of Shu filled the sky with flying swords, and modern xianxia, "immortal hero" stories, carried them around the world. In these tales a disciple joins a sect on a misty mountain and spends years in meditation, feeding a sword with their own qi until blade and spirit are bound together. Then the sword answers a thought like a hand. It leaps from its sheath, circles its master in a ribbon of light, strikes far beyond sight and comes home again. The greatest cultivators even stand on their blades and ride them across the sea of clouds, from one peak to the next.

## beats

- meditate: A cultivator floats cross-legged among the floating peaks, gathering qi above a sea of clouds at dawn.
- summon: At a single thought, the flying sword slips free of its sheath and hangs glowing in the air.
- circle: The sword orbits its master in a ribbon of blue qi, bound to the cultivator's spirit.
- launch: At a silent command the blade streaks away and vanishes past the edge of the sky.
- cut: Far off, the flying sword meets a falling rock and splits it cleanly in two.
- sheathe: The sword returns in a streak of light and slides home into its sheath while the cultivator meditates on.
