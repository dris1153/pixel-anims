## legend

Long ago the devas and the asuras fought for a hundred years, and the devas lost. Mahishasura, the buffalo demon, lord of the asuras, drove the gods out of heaven, sat on Indra's throne and seized the powers of the sun, the moon, fire, wind and water. The gods wandered the earth like mortals, until Brahma led them to Vishnu and Shiva.

Hearing their tale, Vishnu and Shiva frowned in fury. From their faces, and from Brahma's, blazed a great radiance, the tejas, and more streamed from Indra and every other god. The lights flowed together into one mountain of fire, and out of it took shape a woman whose glow filled the three worlds: the Goddess, Devi, whom the world knows as Durga.

Each god gave her a weapon drawn from his own: Shiva a trident, Vishnu a discus, Varuna a conch and a noose, Agni a spear, Vayu a bow and arrows, Indra a thunderbolt and a bell from his elephant Airavata, and Kala, Time himself, a sword and shield. Himavat, king of the mountains, gave her a lion to ride. Then she laughed aloud, again and again, and the earth quaked, the seas heaved and the mountains rocked.

The asura armies rushed at the sound, led by the generals Chikshura and Chamara, but the Goddess cut them down and her lion swept through them like fire through a forest. Then Mahishasura came himself as a giant buffalo. He pounded the earth with his hooves, lashed the sea with his tail and tossed mountains with his horns.

She caught him in her noose, and he became a lion. As she moved to strike, he became a man with a sword, then a great elephant that dragged at her lion with its trunk, until her sword drove it off. Then he was a buffalo again, bellowing until the three worlds shook. The Goddess leapt onto him, pinned his neck beneath her foot and struck with her trident. Half out of the buffalo's mouth he came, still fighting, and there she slew him. His armies fled, the gods sang her praises, and she promised to come again whenever they called on her in danger.

Every autumn, in the month of Ashvin, Bengal welcomes her home. In Kumartuli, on the Hooghly in Kolkata, artisans shape her from straw and river clay, and when her eyes are painted, the clay is said to come alive. For days she sits in glowing pandals while dhak drums thunder and dancers swing smoking dhunuchi burners. Then on Vijaya Dashami she is carried to the river and immersed, as the crowd calls "Asche bochhor abar hobe!": next year, it will happen again.

## beats

- idol: At autumn dusk in a bamboo workshop by the river, an artisan paints the eyes of a clay Durga, and they begin to glow.
- conquest: The glow opens into the legend: Mahishasura's horned army storms Indra's heaven and the gods flee across the golden clouds.
- gods: Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva stand in fury, and a fiery radiance streams from their brows.
- tejas: The radiance of all the gods pours together into one blazing mountain of light.
- durga: From the light the Goddess takes form, ten-armed, in a red sari and golden crown, with a third eye on her brow.
- weapons: God after god hands her a weapon, trident, discus, conch, thunderbolt, bow and sword, each flashing into one of her hands.
- lion: A lion bounds down from the snowy peaks of Himavat; she mounts it and laughs, and the worlds tremble.
- army: The demon army charges, her lion leaps among them, and her arrows and whirling discus scatter them.
- buffalo: Mahishasura charges as a giant buffalo, head lowered, tossing mountains with his horns.
- shapes: He changes shape again and again, a lion, a swordsman, an elephant dragging at her lion, then the buffalo once more.
- slay: She pins him with her foot, and as he rises half out of the buffalo her trident strikes; he falls in a burst of light.
- praise: The gods shower flowers, conches sound, and all of heaven rejoices at her victory.
- pandal: The vision folds back into the idol, now enthroned in a glowing festival pandal as dhak drummers beat.
- dhunuchi: Dancers swing smoking clay incense burners in circles to the rhythm of the drums.
- visarjan: At dusk the idol rides down to the river on shoulders among lamps and cheers, as the crowd cries "Asche bochhor abar hobe!"
- return: The idol sinks and its clay melts into the ripples, while on the bank a new idol waits for its eyes.
