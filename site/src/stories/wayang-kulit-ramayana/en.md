origin: Indonesia · The Ramayana as told in Javanese wayang kulit shadow play
---

## legend

On the night of a wedding or a village feast in Java, people gather before a great white screen. Behind it sits the dalang, the puppet master, under a burning oil lamp, with flat puppets of buffalo hide at his side, carved as fine as lace and stuck upright in a banana trunk. Beside him waits the gamelan, its gongs and bronze keys shining in the lamplight. When he is ready, the dalang lifts the gunungan, a leaf-shaped puppet that stands for the mountain and the tree of life, and sets it trembling in the middle of the screen. The story is about to begin.

Tonight it is the Ramayana. Prince Rama, sent away from his kingdom, lives in the forest with his wife Sita, whom the Javanese call Sinta, and his loyal brother Laksmana. One day a golden deer leaps past, so beautiful that Sita begs Rama to catch it for her. He follows it deep into the trees. But the deer is a demon in disguise, sent to lure him away.

While Rama is gone, Rahwana, the demon king of Alengka, comes for Sita. He seizes her and carries her off through the sky, across the sea to his island kingdom.

Rama searches everywhere for her. The monkey king Sugriwa lends him his army, and the white monkey Hanoman leaps across the sea and finds Sita in Rahwana's garden. To carry the army over the water, the monkeys hurl rock after rock into the sea until they have built a bridge all the way to Alengka.

Then comes the great war. Arrows fly, giants fall, and the gamelan pounds. At last Rama draws his bow, and his arrow strikes Rahwana down. Sita walks through fire to prove she has stayed true, the flames do not touch her, and she comes home to Rama.

All through the night the dalang does it alone. He moves every puppet and speaks every voice, prince and demon, monkey and princess, sings the songs that set each mood, and taps on his puppet chest to tell the gamelan when to rise and when to fall. Only when the tale is done does he plant the gunungan in the center of the screen once more. By then the lamp is burning low and the sky is growing pale, for a wayang kulit performance lasts until dawn.

## beats

- gunungan: The leaf-shaped gunungan quivers in the middle of the lamplit screen, opening the night's play.
- forest: Rama and Sita walk through a shadow forest as the golden deer leaps past, still gleaming with its gilt.
- abduct: The demon king Rahwana swoops in, huge and crowned, with bulging eyes, and carries Sita away.
- army: Monkey warriors leap across the screen, piling up rocks into the bridge to Alengka.
- battle: Rama and Rahwana clash, their arrows flying as streaks of shadow while the gongs pound.
- victory: Rama's arrow strikes home, Rahwana topples off the screen, and Sita returns to his side.
- close: The gunungan returns and spins to close the story, and the gamelan players bow.
- dawn: The lamp burns low as dawn pales over the palms, then flares up again for the next night's play.
