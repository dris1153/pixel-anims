## legend

Long before anyone lived in the heart of Anatolia, the land was shaped by fire. Great volcanoes, Erciyes, Hasan and others, erupted again and again and buried the plateau under deep layers of ash. The ash settled and hardened into tuff, a soft stone in shades of cream and pink, and in places a harder, darker rock lay over it like a lid.

Then came the slow work of water and wind. Rain, snowmelt and frost carved the soft tuff into valleys, but wherever a hard cap sat on top, it sheltered the stone beneath like an umbrella. Over ages the land wore away around them and left those sheltered columns standing: cones, towers and pillars wearing dark stone hats. The people call them peri bacaları, fairy chimneys, for it was once said that fairies lived inside.

In truth, people lived inside. The tuff is soft enough to carve with simple tools, and for many centuries the people of Cappadocia dug homes, storerooms and pigeon houses into the rock. Early Christians cut churches and monasteries into the cliffs and painted their walls with saints, and whole cities were dug many storeys underground, where villagers could hide from raiders. In the valleys, vineyards and orchards grew in the rich volcanic soil, as they still do.

Today the valleys are famous for another sight. Since the 1990s, hot-air balloons have risen here on almost every calm morning. The crews set out in the dark, because the air is coolest and stillest just before sunrise. Fans blow cold air into the envelopes as they lie on the ground, then the burners roar, and the heated air lifts them upright, glowing like lanterns.

A balloon has no steering wheel. It can only rise and sink, so the pilot reads the winds, which blow in different directions at different heights, climbing to catch one and dropping to find another. In the sheltered valleys the pilots can sink low enough to skim the treetops and drift between the chimneys, then climb high over the plateau as the sun comes up. On the finest mornings more than a hundred balloons fill the sky at once above the glowing pink rock, before they settle back to earth, one by one, and the day begins.

## beats

- predawn: In the blue hour three striped envelopes lie flat beside their baskets while lamps glow in the cave windows.
- inflate: Fans blow the envelopes half full, then the burners roar and they stand upright, glowing like lanterns.
- liftoff: The balloons lift one by one, passengers waving from the baskets, as the sky begins to blush.
- sunrise: The sun breaks over the mesas, the fairy chimneys glow pink and gold, and dozens of far balloons drift up.
- drift: The middle balloon dips low over the apricot trees, brushing leaves loose, then fires its burner and climbs.
- land: The balloons settle back onto the field and the envelopes sigh flat as the light cools to the blue hour.
