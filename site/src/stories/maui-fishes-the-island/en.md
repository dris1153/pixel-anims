## legend

Māui was the youngest of five brothers, and the cleverest, and the most trouble. His brothers were tired of him. When they went fishing, they left him behind on the shore, and so he learned to go fishing his own way.

He had a secret. His grandmother, Murirangawhenua, had given him her jawbone, and from it he carved a fishhook, and that hook was full of her power. One dawn, before his brothers launched their waka, Māui slipped aboard and hid under the floorboards.

They paddled far out, until the land was a thin line behind them, before Māui popped up from his hiding place. The brothers were furious and wanted to turn back, but Māui made the shore seem so far away that they gave up and kept going. When they reached the fishing grounds, he asked for bait. They refused him. So Māui struck his own nose, smeared the hook with his blood, and cast his line down into the deep.

The line went tight. The waka tipped and the sea boiled around it. Māui hauled, and chanted a karakia to lighten the load, and his brothers, terrified, pulled with him. Slowly, out of the water, rose a fish so vast that its back stretched across the whole horizon.

Māui told his brothers to wait while he went to make an offering to the gods, and not to touch the fish until he came back. But as soon as he was gone they leapt onto it with their knives and began to cut it up, each one wanting the best part. The fish thrashed under their blades, and where they hacked, it was left in valleys, mountains, lakes and cliffs.

The fish is still there. It is the North Island of New Zealand, Te Ika-a-Māui, the fish of Māui. Its head lies at Wellington, its tail far away in the north. The South Island is his canoe, Te Waka-a-Māui. And Rakiura, Stewart Island, is the anchor stone that held the waka steady while Māui hauled up a whole land from the sea.

## beats

- shore: At dawn Māui's brothers push their waka off, not seeing Māui hidden under the floorboards.
- reveal: Far out at sea Māui pops up, and his brothers shake their paddles at him.
- hook: He holds up the magic fishhook carved from his grandmother's jawbone.
- bait: He baits the hook with his own blood and casts the glowing line into the deep.
- bite: The line goes taut, the waka tips and the sea swirls.
- haul: Māui and his brothers haul, and a vast fish rises across the whole horizon.
- island: The fish becomes an island, water pouring from it in waterfalls.
- carve: The brothers hack at the fish, carving valleys, mountains and lakes.
- green: Forest spreads over the island as the Matariki stars rise.
- return: Sea mist rolls in, the island sinks back into legend and the waka rests on the shore again.
