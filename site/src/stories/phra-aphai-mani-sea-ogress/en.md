origin: Thailand · Sunthorn Phu's epic poem Phra Aphai Mani, 19th century

## legend

Long ago, King Suthat of the city of Rattana sent his two sons away to study. The younger, Si Suwan, learned to fight. But the elder, Phra Aphai Mani, learned only one thing: to play the pi, a pipe whose music could soothe any heart, lull every listener to sleep, and even take a life. When they came home, their father was furious. What use was a pipe to a future king? He drove them both out of the kingdom.

The brothers wandered down to the seashore. There, one night, Phra Aphai Mani played for three young brahmins they had met on the road, and one by one his listeners fell asleep. But the music drifted far out over the water, and in the deep the sea ogress Phisuea Samut heard it. She was a giant of the ocean, dark and terrible, and the song filled her with love. She rose from the waves, seized the sleeping prince and carried him off to her cave beneath the sea.

To win his heart she took the shape of a beautiful woman, and the prince lived with her for years. They had a son, Sinsamut, who had his mother's giant strength. But Phra Aphai Mani never stopped longing for the world above, and he never forgot what she truly was.

At last a family of merfolk agreed to help him. While the ogress was away, they carried the prince and his son across the sea to the island of Ko Kaew Phitsadan, where a hermit lived. The ogress came raging after them, but the hermit's power kept her from setting foot on the shore, and she could only weep and rage at the water's edge.

Phra Aphai Mani married the mermaid who had saved him, and she bore him a son, Sutsakhon. Yet the ogress never gave up. When at last she came after him once more across the open sea, he raised his pipe and played her one final song, and its music took her life.

Many people say that Ko Kaew Phitsadan is Ko Samet in the Gulf of Thailand, where statues of the prince with his pipe and the mermaid now stand beside the sea.

## beats

- song: Phra Aphai Mani sits on the hermit's rock in the moonlight, and golden rings of music roll out over the sea.
- lure: Charmed by the song, a beautiful woman rises from the moon path and glides toward the shore.
- reveal: Halfway in she swells into the giant sea ogress Phisuea Samut, and the moon turns blood red.
- escape: A mermaid bursts from the surf and the prince leaps onto her back, fleeing as he did in the poem.
- chase: The ogress wades after them, throwing up walls of water, and hurls a boulder that crashes beside them.
- last song: The prince turns and plays his final sorrowful song, and golden light wraps around the ogress.
- stone: She sinks to her knees and hardens into a stone islet as the moon fades back to silver.
- return: The mermaid carries him back to the rock, the islet sinks beneath the tide, and he lifts his pipe once more.
