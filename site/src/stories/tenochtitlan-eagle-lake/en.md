## legend

Long ago the Mexica people left their homeland of Aztlán, far to the north, and set out on a journey that would last for generations. Their god Huitzilopochtli, the hummingbird of the south, travelled with them as a sacred bundle carried by his priests, and he made them a promise. Keep walking, he told them, until you see an eagle perched on a nopal cactus, devouring a serpent. There you will build your city, and it will be great.

They walked for many years. They were chased from one place to the next, lived as servants of other peoples and slept in the reeds. At last they came to the shores of Lake Texcoco, in a valley ringed by volcanoes. And there, on a small rocky island in the middle of the lake, they saw it: a nopal heavy with red fruit, and on it a great eagle with its wings spread against the sun, holding a snake in its beak.

It was not much of a place to build a city. The island was small and swampy, and the lake around it was salty. But the Mexica had been promised, and so they began.

They wove rafts of reeds, heaped them with mud from the lake bed, and planted them with maize, beans, squash and flowers: the chinampas, floating gardens that grew the island outward, field by field. They built stone causeways to the shores and dug canals that canoes could glide along. At the heart of the city they raised the Templo Mayor, a great stepped pyramid with two shrines on top, one for Huitzilopochtli and one for Tlaloc, god of rain.

In time Tenochtitlan became one of the largest cities in the world. Its market at Tlatelolco sold cacao, feathers, cloth and gold, and every fifty-two years, when the calendar completed its great cycle, every fire in the land was put out, and the priests lit a new fire on a hilltop, and runners carried it back across the causeways to every house.

Today the city on the lake is Mexico City. And the eagle on the cactus with the serpent in its beak is at the centre of the Mexican flag.

## beats

- wander: At dawn Mexica travellers walk the shore, a priest carrying the sacred bundle of Huitzilopochtli.
- omen: On the island an eagle lands on a nopal cactus, a serpent in its beak, wings spread against the sun.
- island: The travellers pole reed canoes across and plant a banner beside the cactus.
- chinampas: They weave floating gardens around the island, and rows of maize and marigolds sprout.
- causeway: Stone causeways and canals reach out to the shores, and laden canoes glide along them.
- temple: The Templo Mayor rises step by step, with twin shrines of red and blue.
- market: At noon the Tlatelolco market fills with cloth, cacao and feathers, and dancers whirl.
- newfire: At night every fire goes out, then the New Fire is kindled on a hill and torches race home.
- city: At dawn the island city gleams white and red on the blue lake.
- return: Morning mist takes the city back to bare rock and reeds, and the travellers appear again.
