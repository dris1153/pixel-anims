## legend

In the days of the sixth Hùng king, in the village of Gióng, lived an old couple who longed for a child. One day the wife saw a huge footprint in the fields and set her own foot in it to see how much bigger it was. Soon after, she found she was with child, and twelve months later she gave birth to a fine, handsome boy.

But the boy was strange. At three years old he still could not speak, could not laugh and could not walk. Wherever he was laid down, there he stayed.

Then the Ân invaders crossed into the land. Their army was strong, and the king sent envoys to every village to call for anyone who could save the country. When the boy heard the envoy's cry, he spoke for the very first time: "Mother, ask the envoy to come in." And to the envoy he said: "Tell the king to make me an iron horse, an iron rod and a suit of iron armor, and I will drive these invaders away."

From that day the boy grew so fast you could almost watch it happen. However much rice he ate, he was still hungry, and every new shirt split at the seams. His parents could not feed him alone, so the whole village gladly brought rice to help.

When the enemy reached the foot of Trâu mountain, the envoy arrived with the iron horse, the iron rod and the armor. The boy sprang up, stretched once, and became a towering warrior. He put on the armor, took up the rod and leapt onto the horse. The horse breathed fire, and they charged straight at the enemy, cutting them down rank after rank.

Suddenly the iron rod snapped. Without slowing, the warrior tore up clumps of bamboo by the roadside and swept them into the enemy. The invaders broke and fled, trampling one another. He chased them to the foot of Sóc mountain, rode alone to the top, took off his iron armor, and horse and rider rose slowly into the sky.

The king named him Phù Đổng Thiên Vương and built a temple in his village, where a great festival is held every year in the fourth lunar month. People say the golden đằng ngà bamboo got its color from the horse's fiery breath, the hoofprints became a chain of ponds, and a village the fire swept through is still called Làng Cháy, the Burnt Village.

## beats

- still: A three-year-old boy lies silent on his mat in the hut doorway as the invaders' smoke creeps closer.
- call: A war drum booms across the paddies, the call for a hero, and the boy who never spoke sits up and lifts his fist.
- grow: He grows in three great jolts, bursting the thatch off the roof, until he stands twice the height of the hut.
- forge: Iron armor, an iron rod and a fire-breathing iron horse arrive, and he vaults onto its back.
- charge: The horse breathes flame across the paddies, he strikes at the enemy, and the iron rod snaps.
- bamboo: He tears up a whole bamboo clump and sweeps the enemy away, the horse's fire scorching the stalks gold.
- ascend: Galloping to the mountain, he sheds his armor, and horse and rider climb into the clouds on a trail of light.
- return: The bamboo turns green, the hut is whole again, and the small boy lies silent on his mat once more.
