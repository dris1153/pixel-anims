## legend

When King Uther Pendragon died, the land had no king, and the lords quarrelled over the crown. Then, one Christmas, a great stone appeared in a London churchyard, with an anvil of steel on top and a sword driven through the anvil. Letters of gold said that whoever pulled the sword out was the rightful king of all England. Knight after knight heaved at it, and the sword did not move.

At New Year a boy named Arthur, squire to his foster brother Sir Kay, was sent to fetch a sword Kay had forgotten. Finding no one home, he ran to the churchyard and drew the sword from the stone as lightly as from a sheath. He was no ordinary boy. He was Uther's own son, whom Merlin the wizard had hidden away as a baby, and soon he was crowned king.

When his first sword broke in battle, Merlin led him to a lake. From the middle of the water rose an arm clothed in white samite, holding a sword. Arthur rowed out and took it, and the arm sank back beneath the water. This was Excalibur, the gift of the Lady of the Lake.

At Camelot Arthur gathered the noblest knights in the world around the Round Table. One Pentecost, as they sat at supper, thunder cracked, a sunbeam brighter than day filled the hall, and the Holy Grail passed through it, covered in white samite, and was gone. The knights vowed to seek it, and rode away on the great quest, and many never came home.

So the fellowship broke apart. While Arthur was away at war, his son Mordred seized the kingdom. They met in a last battle, remembered as Camlann, where nearly every knight on both sides was slain. Arthur ran Mordred through, but Mordred, with his last strength, gave the king a mortal wound.

The dying king told Sir Bedivere to throw Excalibur into the lake. Twice Bedivere could not bear to, and hid it. The third time he threw it, and an arm rose from the water, caught it by the hilt, brandished it three times and drew it under. Then a barge glided to the shore bearing three queens in black hoods, and they carried Arthur away over the water to the Isle of Avalon, to heal him of his wound.

Some say he died there. Others say he is only sleeping and will come again when Britain needs him most, for on his tomb, they say, is written: Here lies Arthur, the once and future king.

## beats

- stone: On a snowy winter night the sword in the anvil glows faintly silver while candlelight flickers in the chapel window.
- trial: Armoured knights heave at the hilt one after another and stagger back; the sword does not move.
- arthur: A boy squire draws the sword in one easy pull, and a pillar of white light bursts from the stone.
- crowned: Merlin appears in a puff of smoke and sets a golden crown on the boy's head as he grows into a young king.
- excalibur: Arthur rows out, and an arm in white samite rises from the lake to hand him Excalibur.
- camelot: Spring melts the snow, and the white walls and towers of Camelot rise on the hill with red banners unfurling.
- grail: On a summer evening the sky opens above Camelot and the Holy Grail floats in a beam of light.
- mordred: Storm clouds roll in at dusk as Mordred, in black armour under a red and black banner, rides toward the king.
- camlann: King and traitor clash on the hillside until Mordred falls and Arthur sinks to one knee, wounded.
- bedivere: Sir Bedivere hurls Excalibur over the lake, and the white arm catches it, brandishes it three times and draws it under.
- avalon: A black barge with three veiled queens carries the king into the mist toward Avalon as Camelot crumbles.
- return: Winter returns, the ruins fade into the hill, and a silver light rises from the lake to become the sword in the stone again.
