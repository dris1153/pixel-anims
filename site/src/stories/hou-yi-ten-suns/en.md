---
origin: China · Ancient myth, told in the Huainanzi and in Mid-Autumn folk tradition

## legend

At the eastern edge of the world, where the sea meets the sky, grows the Fusang tree, a mulberry so tall its branches brush the clouds. In its branches lived the ten suns, children of the god of the eastern sky, each a golden crow with three legs, wrapped in a ring of fire. Every morning one of them flew up to cross the sky while the other nine rested, and so the world had one day at a time.

But one morning the ten grew bored of waiting their turns. They flew up together, all ten at once, and the sky burned white. Rivers dried to cracked mud, crops withered in the fields, and people hid in the dark of their houses, afraid to step outside.

Then the archer Hou Yi climbed a mountain with a red bow and a quiver of white arrows. He drew and let fly, and the first sun burst into sparks and fell to earth as a black three-legged crow. He shot again, and again, until nine crows lay on the ground and a single sun hung in a blue sky. That one he spared, so the world would still have light. The clouds came back, and with them the rain.

For saving the world, the Queen Mother of the West gave Hou Yi an elixir of immortality. He did not drink it, because he could not bear to live forever without his wife, Chang'e, and he gave it to her to keep.

One night, while Hou Yi was away, his faithless pupil Peng Meng came to steal the elixir. Chang'e could not fight him off, so she swallowed it herself. At once she grew light as mist and began to rise, out of the house, over the ridge, up and up until she came to rest on the moon. There she lives still, in the cold Palace of Guanghan, under an osmanthus tree, with only the Jade Rabbit for company, pounding medicine in its mortar.

Hou Yi, grieving, set out her favourite fruits and cakes in the moonlight. The people did the same, and so, every year on the fifteenth night of the eighth month, when the moon is fullest, families still gather with mooncakes and lanterns and look up to find her.

## beats

- fusang: At dawn the ten suns perch in the Fusang tree and one flies up to cross the sky, as it should.
- ten: All ten suns fly up at once and the sky blazes white-gold.
- scorch: The river dries, the rice withers and the farmers run inside.
- archer: Hou Yi strides up the ridge with his red bow and draws.
- volley: Each arrow strikes a sun, which falls as a black crow, until only one remains.
- rain: Clouds roll in, rain pours and the valley turns green again.
- elixir: The Queen Mother of the West hands Hou Yi the elixir, and he carries it home to Chang'e.
- thief: A thief creeps up by night, and Chang'e swallows the elixir so he cannot take it.
- flight: Chang'e floats up toward the full moon, her sleeves and ribbons streaming.
- moon: The moon swells close: the Palace of Guanghan, the osmanthus tree and the Jade Rabbit.
- lanterns: Hou Yi lays out mooncakes and incense and red lanterns drift up from the village.
- return: At dawn the fallen crows fly home to the Fusang tree and relight, so the tale can begin again.
