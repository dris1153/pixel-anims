origin: Korea · Founding myth of Goguryeo, told in the Samguk Sagi, the Samguk Yusa and the Lay of King Dongmyeong

## legend

Long ago Haemosu, son of the Emperor of Heaven, came down to earth in a chariot drawn by five dragons. His followers rode behind him on white swans, and on his head he wore a crown of crow feathers.

By the river he met Yuhwa, eldest daughter of Habaek, the god of the river. Haemosu won her heart, but then he went back up to Heaven and never returned. Her father was furious that she had gone with a man without his blessing, and he drove her out of his waters.

Fishermen found her and brought her to King Geumwa of Buyeo, who shut her away in a room of his palace. But a beam of sunlight found her there. When she stepped aside, it followed her. Soon after, Yuhwa laid a great egg.

The king thought it an evil omen. He threw it to the dogs and pigs, but they would not touch it. He left it on the road, but the cattle and horses stepped around it. He cast it out into the fields, and the birds came down and covered it with their wings. He could not even break it, so at last he gave it back to its mother. She wrapped it up and kept it warm, and a boy broke out of the shell.

At seven the boy made his own bow and arrows, and he never missed what he aimed at. In Buyeo a fine archer was called "jumong", and so that became his name. The king's seven sons grew jealous and plotted to kill him, and his mother warned him to flee. Jumong rode south with three loyal friends, and soldiers rode after them.

At a wide river there was no bridge and no boat, and the riders were close behind. Jumong struck the water with his bow and cried, "I am the son of Heaven and the grandson of the river god! My enemies are almost upon me. What shall I do?" At once fish and turtles rose to the surface and lay side by side to make a bridge. Jumong and his friends crossed, and the moment they reached the far bank the bridge melted away, and the riders could only stop at the water's edge.

Among the green hills of Jolbon, Jumong founded a new kingdom and called it Goguryeo. He ruled it until he was forty, and then, the old books say, he rose up to Heaven and did not come back. Later storytellers said he rode there on a kirin.

## beats

- descent: At dawn the clouds part and Haemosu comes down in his five-dragon chariot, trailed by swans, crowned with crow feathers.
- river daughter: Yuhwa rises from the river to meet him, the water churns in anger, and Haemosu soars away alone.
- sun egg: A beam of sunlight follows Yuhwa wherever she walks, and in its glow a great egg appears in her arms.
- guardians: In a cold wind white birds circle down and spread their wings over the egg as Yuhwa fades into the river.
- hatch: The egg cracks in golden light, and the boy who steps out grows into Jumong, the young archer.
- archery: Jumong's arrows flash one after another through a jade ring hung from a far pine, setting it spinning.
- pursuit: At dusk Buyeo horsemen with torches and spears pour over the ridge and drive Jumong to the river's edge.
- bridge: Under the moon Jumong strikes the water with his bow, and fish and turtles lock into a glowing bridge.
- goguryeo: The bridge scatters, the riders rein up, and at sunrise the walls of Goguryeo rise beneath a three-legged crow.
- return: Jumong rides a white kirin into the morning sky, and the valley empties, ready for the tale to begin anew.
