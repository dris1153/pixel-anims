## legend

In 1902, in a glass-walled studio just outside Paris, a stage magician named Georges Méliès made a film unlike anything seen before. It ran for about a quarter of an hour, very long for its day, and it was full of painted scenery, trick effects and dancers from the Paris stage. Some copies were even coloured by hand, frame by frame. Méliès himself played the hero.

The film opens at a grand meeting of astronomers, all in long starry robes and tall pointed hats, whose telescopes turn into stools in a flash. Their president, Professor Barbenfouillis, rises and proposes a daring plan: a journey to the Moon. The hall erupts. One old astronomer objects so loudly that the professor flings a pile of papers at his head. In the end the professor and five of his colleagues are chosen for the voyage.

The travellers visit the workshop where their vehicle is being built, a shell shaped like a giant bullet. From a rooftop among the smoking chimneys they watch a huge cannon being cast. Then a troupe of young women in sailor suits helps the explorers into the shell and pushes it into the cannon's mouth. The signal is given, the cannon roars, and the shell shoots off into the sky.

The Moon grows closer and closer, and it has a face. The shell lands right in its eye, and the poor Moon grimaces.

On the lunar ground the explorers watch the Earth rise, then lie down under their blankets to sleep. In their dreams the stars show the faces of women, and old Saturn leans out of his ring. Snow falls and wakes them. They climb into a crater and find a cave of giant mushrooms, where the Selenites, the people of the Moon, leap out at them, nimble as insects. But a Selenite struck with an umbrella vanishes in a puff of smoke. The explorers are captured and dragged before the Selenite king, but they break free and race back to the shell.

The professor tips it over the edge of the Moon, and it falls all the way back to Earth, splashing into the sea and sinking to the bottom before it bobs up again. A ship tows it to harbour, and the heroes come home to a parade, medals and crowns of laurel.

The film was copied and shown all over the world, and its Moon with a shell in its eye became one of the most famous pictures in the history of cinema.

## beats

- congress: Four astronomers in starry robes argue on the rooftop, and their telescopes turn into stools in a puff.
- cannon: Chorus girls in sailor suits roll the bullet-shaped shell across the roof and push it, astronomers inside, into the cannon.
- fire: A girl waves the signal flag, the cannon fires in a huge cloud of smoke, and the shell streaks toward the Moon.
- eye: The Moon's face swells close until the shell plunges into its eye and it grimaces.
- dream: On the lunar ground the explorers sleep while star-women look down, Saturn peers out of his ring and snow falls.
- selenites: Selenites leap out of a mushroom grotto, burst into smoke at a bop of an umbrella, and the explorers run.
- fall: The shell tips over the Moon's edge, falls past streaming stars and splashes down among fish and bubbles.
- parade: Back on the rooftop the heroes bow in laurel crowns under confetti, and the Moon slides back into place, smiling.
