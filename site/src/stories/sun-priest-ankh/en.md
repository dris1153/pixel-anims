## legend

To the ancient Egyptians, nothing mattered more than the sun. They called it Ra, the great god who made the world and lit it anew every day. At dawn he was Khepri, the scarab pushing the young sun over the horizon. At noon he was Ra in all his strength, sailing across the sky in his golden boat. At evening he was Atum, the old one, going down into the west to travel through the underworld and be born again the next morning.

Ra's holiest city was Iunu, which the Greeks called Heliopolis, the City of the Sun. There, the priests taught, the first mound of earth rose out of the dark waters at the beginning of time, and there the sun first appeared. A sacred pointed stone, the benben, stood in the temple to remember that moment. Its shape was echoed in the capstones of the pyramids and in the tall stone obelisks that stood in pairs before the temples. The obelisks were carved with hieroglyphs praising the sun and the king, and their tips were often sheathed in gold or electrum, so they caught the first light of morning.

The priests of Ra served their god day and night. They shaved their heads, washed many times a day and dressed in clean white linen. Before dawn they sang hymns to greet the rising sun, and at evening they sang again as it went down into the west.

In the hands of the gods, in painting after painting, is the ankh: a cross with a loop at the top. It was the hieroglyph for the word life. Gods are shown holding it to a pharaoh's face to give him the breath of life, and ordinary people wore it as an amulet and carved it on mirrors, jewellery and tombs.

For the Egyptians, the sun and the ankh told the same story. Each night the world went dark, and each morning Ra rose again and gave life back to everything under the sky. As long as the priests kept their hymns and the sun kept its path, the world would go on.

## beats

- idle: At dusk a priest kneels before a carved obelisk in the desert temple, the pyramids dark behind him.
- raise: He lifts a golden ankh high in both hands, offering the sign of life up to Ra.
- dawn: The sun disc rises behind the pyramids and the hieroglyphs on the obelisk light up one by one.
- beam: A beam of sunlight strikes the golden ankh, as if Ra himself were answering with life.
- fade: The glow fades from the ankh and the hieroglyphs as the sun's light draws away.
- bow: The priest bows low in thanks, and the temple settles back into the quiet of dusk.
