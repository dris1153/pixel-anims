## legend

In the northern isles of Scotland, the old folk say, the seals are not always seals. They are the selkie folk, and on certain nights, midsummer among them, they swim to lonely beaches, slip off their sealskins and come ashore as men and women, fairer than any on land, to dance on the sand under the pale northern sky.

One such night a young fisherman came down to the shore and saw them dancing, their skins lying in a heap on the rocks. He crept down, snatched one skin and hid it. When the dancers saw him they ran for their skins and plunged back into the sea, all but one. She searched the shore, weeping, for without her skin she could never go home.

The fisherman spoke to her gently, and she had nowhere else to go. So she followed him to his cottage and became his wife. She was a good wife, they say, and bore him children, and she kept the house and mended the nets. But she seldom laughed, and many an evening she walked alone by the water, gazing out over the grey sea as if she heard someone calling.

He had hidden the skin well, up in the thatch of the roof. But years later one of the children found it, a soft and silvery thing, and brought it to her mother to ask what it was. The moment she held it to her face, all her longing came back at once.

She kissed her children goodbye, ran down to the shore, pulled on the skin and dived into the waves. The fisherman, rowing home, saw a great bull seal rise to meet her, her own husband from the sea, who had waited for her all those years. From the water she called back to him: "Farewell, and fare you well. I liked you well, for you were good to me, but I loved my man of the sea better."

She never came back to live on land. But some say she was often seen near that shore, watching over her children from the waves, and in some tellings fish were left on the rocks for them, a gift from the sea.

## beats

- seals: Under the pale midsummer midnight, seals haul out on the skerries, barking, while the sea shines.
- shed: The seals slip off their silver skins and become dark-haired women dancing in a ring on the sand.
- hide: A young fisherman creeps out and hides one sealskin in his thatch; the others dive, leaving one woman alone.
- bride: The seasons pass over the cottage as she hangs nets and walks the shore, always gazing out to sea.
- found: A gust lifts the thatch and the skin drops out, glowing silver, and she holds it to her face.
- sea: She runs down to the shore, pulls on her skin and dives in as a seal.
- farewell: A great bull seal rises beside her; she looks back once, and a fresh fish lands on the cottage step.
- return: Midsummer comes round again and the seals haul out on the skerries, just as on the night it began.
