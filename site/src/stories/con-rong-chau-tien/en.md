## legend

Long ago, in the land of the Lạc Việt, there lived a god of the dragon race named Lạc Long Quân, son of the goddess Long Nữ. He had the body of a dragon and lived mostly under the water, coming up onto the land only now and then. His strength had no equal. He rid the people of three demons that had long preyed on them, Ngư Tinh, Hồ Tinh and Mộc Tinh, and he taught them to farm, to raise animals and how to live. Then he went home to his mother's palace under the sea, and came back whenever he was needed.

High in the mountains of the north lived Âu Cơ, a fairy of the line of Thần Nông, lovely beyond compare. She had heard that the Lạc Việt land was rich in fragrant flowers and rare herbs, so she came to see it. There she met Lạc Long Quân. They fell in love, married, and made their home on land, in the palace of Long Trang.

When her time came, Âu Cơ gave birth to a sac of a hundred eggs, and the eggs hatched into a hundred rosy, beautiful children. They needed no milk and no nursing, yet they grew as if blown up by the wind, handsome and strong as young gods.

But Lạc Long Quân could not stay long on land. Missing the sea, he said goodbye and went back to his water palace. Âu Cơ was left alone with the children, waiting day after day, until she called him and lamented: "Why did you leave me to raise our children alone?"

He came back and said: "I am of the dragon race of the deep waters, and you are of the fairy race of the high mountains. Our ways are not the same, so we cannot live together for long. I will take fifty children down to the sea, and you will take fifty up to the mountains. Whenever there is trouble, help each other, and never forget this promise." And so they parted.

The eldest son, who went with his mother, was made king at Phong Châu. He took the name Hùng Vương and named the country Văn Lang. His civil lords were called Lạc hầu and his war lords Lạc tướng; the king's sons were called quan lang and his daughters mị nương. For many generations every king was called Hùng Vương.

That is why the Vietnamese call one another đồng bào, "of one sac", for they all came from that same sac of a hundred eggs. And still they call themselves the children of the dragon and the grandchildren of the fairy.
