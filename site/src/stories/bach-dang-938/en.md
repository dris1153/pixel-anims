## legend

For a thousand years the land of the Việt had lived under the rule of empires from the north. In the year 938, the Southern Han sent a great fleet to take it back once more, led by the emperor's own son, Prince Liu Hongcao. Their war junks were tall and many. The Việt had small boats, bamboo spears and a general named Ngô Quyền, who knew his river.

The Bạch Đằng river meets the sea between walls of limestone, and twice a day its tide pours in and drains away. When the water is high, the river looks deep and wide enough for any ship. When it runs out, the mud lies bare. Ngô Quyền built his whole battle on that breathing.

At night, at low tide, his soldiers waded into the mud by torchlight and drove rows of great wooden stakes into the riverbed, each one tipped with iron. Then the tide came back and swallowed them, and by morning the river lay calm and innocent, as if nothing had happened.

When the Han fleet sailed in on the high water, a handful of Việt boats rowed out to meet them, fought a little, then turned and fled upstream. The prince gave chase, sure of an easy victory, and his junks sailed straight over the hidden stakes.

Then the tide turned. The water drained away fast, and all around the junks the iron tips rose out of the river. The great hulls came down on them, cracked open and stuck fast, a whole fleet caught in a forest of stakes. From the banks the Việt sent down fire and arrows, and from both sides their boats came pouring out with drums and banners. The fleet was destroyed, and Prince Hongcao died in the river.

The next year Ngô Quyền declared himself king and set his capital at Cổ Loa. A thousand years of northern rule had ended at a single low tide. Generations later, the river's defenders drove stakes into the same mud against new invaders, and the Bạch Đằng won again.

## beats

- stakes: On a moonlit night at low tide, soldiers hammer iron-tipped stakes into the mud while Ngô Quyền keeps watch from the karst.
- flood tide: At dawn the tide rises and hides the stakes; the river looks empty and calm.
- drums: Small Việt boats row out under red banners as bronze drums boom on the bank.
- fleet: The Southern Han war junks sail in from the sea on the high water.
- feint: The small boats skirmish, turn and flee, and the junks chase them right over the stakes.
- ebb: The tide drains away and the iron tips rise among the junks.
- trapped: Hulls crack on the stakes and the whole fleet is stuck fast.
- fire: Burning rafts and fire arrows set the junks alight and the dusk river glows red.
- charge: Việt boats surge out from both banks and the prince's black banner falls into the water.
- victory: At sunrise Ngô Quyền raises his sword on the karst as the smoke drifts away.
- centuries: Tides and years rush past, the stakes wear down into the mud and fishing boats return.
- return: Night falls at low tide and torches appear on the bank, ready for the tale to begin again.
