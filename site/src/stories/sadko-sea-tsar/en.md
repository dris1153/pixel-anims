## legend

In Novgorod the Great there lived a poor gusli player named Sadko. He owned nothing but his gusli, a wing-shaped harp laid across the knees, and he earned his bread playing at rich men's feasts. But one day nobody invited him. So he took his gusli down to the shore of Lake Ilmen, sat on a white stone and played to the water. Twice the lake stirred and he went home afraid. On the third day the Sea Tsar himself rose out of the water.

"Thank you, Sadko of Novgorod," said the Tsar. "You have gladdened my feast. Go and wager your head against the merchants' shops that this lake holds fish with golden fins. Then cast a silk net, and I will send you three such fish." Sadko did just that. The merchants laughed and staked their shops. Sadko cast his net and drew up three fish with fins of gold. He won the shops and grew rich.

Then Sadko built thirty ships and sailed across the sea to trade in foreign lands. But on the way home the ships stopped dead in the open sea. "We have sailed for years," said Sadko, "and never paid the Sea Tsar his tribute." They threw silver and gold into the waves, and still the ships stood. The Tsar wanted a living head.

So they cast lots into the sea. Every lot floated like a duck on the water, but Sadko's went to the bottom like a key. Taking only his gusli, he lay down on an oak plank on the waves, and the freed ships sailed on without him. He fell asleep, and woke at the bottom of the sea, before the Sea Tsar's palace of white stone.

"Play for me," said the Tsar. Sadko played, and the Tsar began to dance. He danced a day, and a second, and a third, and above him the blue sea churned. Ships broke apart and many drowned. The people prayed to St Nicholas.

Then someone touched Sadko on the shoulder. It was St Nicholas, Mikola Mozhaisky, a grey-haired old man. "Break your strings and snap the pegs," he said. "The Tsar will offer you a bride. Let the maidens pass and choose the very last, Chernava. But do not touch her that night, or you will stay in the sea forever."

Sadko obeyed. The strings snapped, the Tsar stopped dancing, and the sea grew still. He chose Chernava and slept beside her without touching her. He woke on the steep bank of the Chernava river outside Novgorod, and his thirty ships were sailing home.

Sadko built a church to St Nicholas, and he never sailed the blue sea again.

## beats

- gusli: At dusk on the shore of Lake Ilmen, poor Sadko sits on his rock playing the gusli, and glittering notes drift over the water.
- tsar: The lake swells and the Sea Tsar rises, a giant with a coral crown and a beard of weed, his green robes streaming.
- fish: Sadko hauls up three golden-finned fish in his silk net, and the gasping merchants on the pier hand over chests of gold.
- fleet: Rich now, Sadko sets out with thirty ships, their carved prows and striped sails crowding toward the horizon.
- becalm: Far out at sea under a leaden sky, the ships stop dead in the water, their sails hanging slack.
- lots: Lots are cast into the sea; the others float like feathers, but Sadko's sinks like a stone.
- plank: Sadko is set down on an oak plank with his gusli, and the freed ships sail on without him.
- sink: The camera sinks with him through the green water, past shafts of light, fish and bubbles, down to the seabed.
- palace: The Sea Tsar's white-stone palace gleams with pearl domes and coral, and pale sea maidens drift past.
- play: Sadko plays his gusli before the palace, and the Sea Tsar begins to dance.
- dance: The Tsar dances wilder and wilder, coral swaying and fish whirling around him.
- storm: The camera rises to show both worlds at once: huge waves, lightning and foundering ships above, the Tsar still dancing below.
- nicholas: St Nicholas, a white-bearded old man with a soft halo, appears beside Sadko; the strings snap, the Tsar stops and the storm falls still.
- bride: The camera sinks back to the palace, the Tsar parades the sea maidens, and Sadko chooses the last, dark-haired Chernava.
- wake: The camera rises with him through a trail of bubbles; he wakes at dawn by Novgorod, and Chernava becomes the river beside him.
- return: His ships come home to the pier, dusk falls, and Sadko sits on his rock playing the gusli again, as the tale begins anew.
