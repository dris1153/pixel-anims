## legend

Dorothy lived in the middle of the great grey Kansas prairie with Uncle Henry and Aunt Em. The grass was grey, the house was grey, and her aunt and uncle had grown grey and never laughed. Only Toto, her little black dog, kept Dorothy from growing grey as well.

One day a cyclone came. Dorothy ran indoors after Toto, and the whole house was lifted up and carried away. When it came down with a bump, she stepped out into a land of flowers and sunshine. The house had landed on the Wicked Witch of the East and killed her. The Munchkins, all dressed in blue, rejoiced, and the Good Witch of the North gave Dorothy the dead witch's silver shoes and kissed her forehead, leaving a round, shining mark to protect her.

To get home, she must ask the great Wizard Oz, who lived in the Emerald City at the end of the road of yellow brick. On the way she lifted a Scarecrow down from his pole, and he came along to ask for brains. She oiled a Tin Woodman who had rusted stiff, and he came to ask for a heart. A Lion bounded out roaring, and when Dorothy slapped his nose he burst into tears: he was a coward, and he wanted courage. In a field of red poppies Dorothy and the Lion sank into a deadly sleep, and an army of field mice pulled the Lion out on a little cart.

In the Emerald City everyone wore green spectacles. The Wizard appeared to Dorothy as an enormous head without a body, and he would help no one until the Wicked Witch of the West was dead. That witch had only one eye, but it saw as far as a telescope. She put on her Golden Cap and called the Winged Monkeys, who carried the friends away. Made to scrub the witch's kitchen, Dorothy lost her temper and threw a bucket of water over her, and the witch melted into a brown puddle.

The great Wizard turned out to be a little old man from Omaha, a humbug. Still, he gave the Scarecrow brains of bran, the Tin Woodman a silk heart and the Lion a drink of courage, then sailed away in his balloon without Dorothy.

At last Glinda, the Good Witch of the South, told her the secret of the silver shoes. Dorothy knocked her heels together three times and said, "Take me home to Aunt Em!" She whirled through the air, the shoes fell off and were lost forever, and she landed on the prairie by the new farmhouse, crying, "I'm so glad to be at home again!"
