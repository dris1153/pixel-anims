## legend

Kay Kavus, King of Iran, was proud and reckless. When a minstrel sang to him of the gardens and treasures of Mazandaran, he would not listen to his elders and marched his army there. But Mazandaran was the land of the divs, the demons, and their lord was the White Div. One night the White Div brought down a black storm of sorcery on the Iranian camp, and when it lifted, the king and his soldiers were blind. The divs threw them into chains.

The news reached old Zal in Zabulistan, and he sent his son Rostam, the greatest champion in Iran. There were two roads to Mazandaran: a long, safe one, and a short one full of terrors. Rostam chose the short road, alone but for his horse Rakhsh, as fierce and loyal as his master. On that road he faced seven labours.

First, Rakhsh killed a lion while Rostam slept. Then, dying of thirst in the desert, Rostam followed a ram to a hidden spring. Third, as he slept by the water, a dragon crept out of the dark. Twice Rakhsh woke him, and twice the dragon vanished by magic, and Rostam scolded his horse. The third time he saw it, and horse and rider killed it together. Fourth, in a green meadow, a beautiful woman came to share his wine and song. But when Rostam spoke the name of God, her face turned black and she showed herself a hideous witch, and he cut her down. Fifth, he captured a warrior named Olad and made him his guide. Sixth, he killed the div chief Arzhang and found the blind king, who told him the only cure: blood from the White Div's heart.

The seventh labour was the White Div himself. Olad led Rostam past the seven mountains to a cave as dark as night. Inside lay the White Div, huge as a mountain, with a black face and white hair. He woke and came at Rostam with a millstone. They fought until the cave shook, and sweat and blood ran to the ground. At last Rostam lifted the giant, dashed him down and drove his dagger into his side. Then he tore out the demon's heart.

He carried it back to Kay Kavus. Three drops of its blood fell into the king's eyes, and his sight returned, bright as the sun. The soldiers were healed the same way, and the whole army cheered its champion.

## beats

- ride: At dusk Rostam rides into Mazandaran on red-roan Rakhsh, in his leopard-skin coat, the ox-headed mace at his saddle.
- dragon: As Rostam sleeps by the spring, Rakhsh rears at the dragon, and Rostam wakes and cuts it down into smoke.
- witch: A lovely woman in silk brings wine and sings, but at the name of God she twists into a hag.
- cave: By night Rostam rolls the stone from the White Div's cave, where glowing eyes watch from the gloom.
- duel: The horned White Div swings his millstone, and they wrestle through cracking rock until Rostam heaves him down.
- heart: Rostam draws out the div's glowing heart, the one cure for the blinded king.
- heal: Three drops touch Kay Kavus's eyes, they shine as his sight returns, and the soldiers cheer.
- return: At dawn Rostam rides Rakhsh out of the mountains, leaving the cave dark and empty behind him.
