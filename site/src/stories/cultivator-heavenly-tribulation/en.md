## legend

For more than two thousand years, seekers in China searched for a way to escape death. Some brewed elixirs of cinnabar and gold in their furnaces. Others turned the furnace inward: through breathing, stillness and meditation they tried to refine the body's own energy, its qi, into something pure and undying. Daoists called this inner alchemy, and its great prize was the golden elixir, formed deep within the body.

Out of these old ideas grew xianxia, stories of immortal heroes. In xianxia novels a cultivator climbs a long ladder of realms: refining qi, building a foundation, forming a golden core, and on toward the nascent soul and beyond. Each step makes them stronger and longer-lived, and each step carries them further from the ordinary world.

But Heaven does not give immortality away. When a cultivator is about to break through to a great new realm, the sky itself answers. Dark clouds gather over the place where they sit, thunder builds, and bolts of heavenly lightning come down one after another, each stronger than the last. This is the heavenly tribulation. If body and will hold firm, the lightning tempers them like steel in a forge, and they rise into the new realm. If they break, nothing may be left but ash.

The idea is older than any modern novel. In Journey to the West, the Patriarch Subhuti warns the young Monkey King that whoever seeks long life will face calamities sent by Heaven: after five hundred years, thunder will come down to strike him, and he must see it coming and escape it. Old folk tales tell of foxes and other creatures who cultivated for centuries, only to hide in terror when the thunder came for them.

So cultivators go to lonely places for their breakthrough: a deep cave, a far desert, a bare mountain summit, away from anyone the lightning might harm. They sit cross-legged, calm their breath, and wait for the storm. And when the last bolt fades and the clouds part, the stars look down on someone who is no longer quite mortal.

## beats

- meditate: The cultivator sits cross-legged on the bare summit, eyes closed, as faint wisps of qi rise into the night.
- gather: Dark clouds spiral overhead into a storm's eye, the wind whips the cultivator's hair, and the palms press together.
- strike: Three purple bolts of heavenly lightning crash down one after another as the cultivator braces beneath them.
- break: Arms flung wide and eyes blazing gold, the cultivator breaks through as a golden core flares in the chest.
- settle: The clouds part to starlight, the glow fades, and the cultivator sits calm again, no longer quite the same.
