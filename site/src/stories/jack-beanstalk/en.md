## legend

There was once a poor widow who lived in a little cottage with her only son, Jack, and all they had in the world was a cow called Milky-white. One morning Milky-white gave no milk at all, so Jack's mother sent him to market to sell her.

On the way Jack met a funny-looking old man, who offered him a swap: the cow for five strange beans. "Plant them overnight," said the man, "and by morning they'll grow right up to the sky." Jack thought that a fine bargain. But when his mother saw the beans, she flew into a rage, flung them out of the window and sent him to bed without any supper.

When Jack woke, his room was strangely dark. Outside, the beans had sprouted into a great beanstalk that climbed up and up until it reached the sky. Jack climbed it, hand over hand, and came out above the clouds on a long road that led to a great house. At the door stood the giant's wife, who gave him breakfast. But then the ground shook, thump, thump, thump, and her husband came home, roaring:

"Fee-fi-fo-fum, I smell the blood of an Englishman. Be he alive, or be he dead, I'll have his bones to grind my bread."

Jack hid in the oven. When the giant had eaten and fallen asleep, Jack crept out, took a bag of gold and slid back down the beanstalk.

When the gold ran out, Jack climbed up again and carried off the giant's hen, which laid a golden egg whenever it was told to lay. Then he went up a third time and took a golden harp that sang all by itself. But as he ran, the harp cried out, "Master! Master!" The giant woke with a roar and came thundering after him, down the beanstalk.

"Mother! Mother! Bring me an axe!" Jack shouted as he reached the ground. He chopped at the stalk until it snapped, and down came the giant, head first, and that was the end of him, with the beanstalk toppling after.

With the hen's golden eggs and the singing harp, Jack and his mother were never poor again, and Jack, they say, married a great princess and lived happily ever after.

## beats

- market: In the morning Jack leads the thin old cow down the lane and trades her to a hooded stranger for five glowing beans.
- thrown: At dusk his furious mother flings the beans out of the window, and they glint in the dark garden.
- sprout: By night a green shoot bursts up and spirals past the moon until the clouds part on the giant's castle.
- climb: At dawn Jack climbs the beanstalk hand over hand, up through the cloud bank to the castle gate.
- giant: The giant stomps out sniffing the air, "Fee-fi-fo-fum", the clouds shaking under him, while Jack hides behind a huge boot.
- treasure: The giant dozes off, and Jack snatches the hen that lays golden eggs and the singing golden harp.
- chase: The harp cries out, the giant wakes with a roar, and he climbs down the shaking beanstalk after Jack.
- axe: Back on the ground Jack chops at the stalk while his mother watches from the door, and wood chips fly.
- fall: The beanstalk topples in a great arc, the giant tumbles off with a crash, and the clouds close over the castle.
- return: Golden eggs heap up by the door, the harp plays, and Jack leads the old cow home, bought back with the gold.
