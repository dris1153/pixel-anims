## legend

In the old stories of Japan, a fox is never just a fox. The kitsune is clever, long-lived and full of magic, and the older it grows, the stronger its magic becomes. A fox that lives long enough learns to take human shape, and as the years pass it grows more tails, one after another, until it has nine. A nine-tailed fox is ancient and wise, and some say it can see and hear anything that happens in the world.

Tales of fox spirits are older than Japan's own books. In China, stories of the nine-tailed fox go back more than two thousand years, and Korea tells of the kumiho. But in Japan the fox found a special place, as the messenger of Inari, the kami of rice and the harvest. Farmers were grateful to foxes because they hunted the mice in the rice fields. At the gates of thousands of Inari shrines stand pairs of stone foxes, and many hold something in their mouths or under a paw: a sheaf of rice, a key, or a round jewel. Some say that jewel holds the fox's power.

On autumn nights, people walking home through the fields sometimes saw strange lights, pale flames that floated and flickered over the stubble where no one could have lit them. They called it kitsunebi, fox-fire. Some said the foxes breathed it out, others that they struck it from their tails. When a whole line of these lights bobbed across a hillside, like lanterns in a procession, people whispered that it was a fox's wedding.

Kitsune can be kind or wicked. Some are loyal guardians, some are faithful wives in human form, and many are tricksters who love to fool the proud and the greedy, leading travellers in circles or paying for their dinner with leaves that look like coins. But every fox has its magic, and a young fox must practise.

So on an autumn evening, when the harvest is in and the sun sinks red over the field, keep an eye on the scarecrow. If a green flame suddenly blazes across the stubble and sends straw flying, you will know that a fox is out there, practising its fox-fire.

## beats

- idle: The fox mage stands in the sunset field, swishing its tail and eyeing the scarecrow across the stubble.
- channel: It leans back and cups its paws, and green fox-fire gathers into a glowing orb as its tail bristles up.
- cast: With a lunge it hurls the orb across the field, and the fox-fire bursts against the scarecrow in a spray of sparks and straw.
- recover: The fox straightens up, tail swaying again, as the scarecrow stops wobbling and the last sparks fade.
