## legend

This is the oldest story ever written down. It begins with a king who had everything and a city with the greatest walls in the world: Gilgamesh of Uruk, two parts god and one part man. He was strong, handsome and restless, and he ruled his people so hard that they prayed to the gods for help.

The gods answered by making him an equal. Out in the wild, among the gazelles, they shaped Enkidu, a man covered in hair who ran with the herds and drank at their waterholes. When at last Enkidu came to Uruk, he met Gilgamesh at the gate, and the two of them wrestled until the doorposts shook and the walls trembled. Neither could win. So they stopped, and laughed, and became the closest of friends.

Together they went looking for glory. They travelled to the Cedar Forest, where the trees rise into the clouds, guarded by the monster Humbaba, whose face was a maze of coils and whose body blazed with seven terrible auras. The sun god Shamash sent thirteen winds to pin him down, and the friends killed him. They cut down the tallest cedar and floated it down the Euphrates as a raft, to make a great door.

Then the goddess Ishtar, angry that Gilgamesh had refused her love, sent the Bull of Heaven down to punish him. With every snort the earth cracked open. But Enkidu seized it by the horns and Gilgamesh drove in his sword.

For that, the gods decided that one of the two friends must die, and they chose Enkidu. Gilgamesh sat by his body for seven days and nights, and for the first time he was afraid of death.

So he set out to find the secret of living for ever. He crossed the Waters of Death with the boatman Urshanabi, to the one man the gods had spared, and learned of a plant at the bottom of the sea that makes the old young again. Gilgamesh tied stones to his feet, sank to the seabed and pulled it up.

But on the way home, while he bathed in a cool pool, a serpent smelled the plant, slid out, swallowed it, and shed its skin, young again. Gilgamesh sat down and wept. Then he went home, stood on the walls of Uruk, and showed the boatman the city he had built, which would outlive him. That was the only kind of for ever there was.
