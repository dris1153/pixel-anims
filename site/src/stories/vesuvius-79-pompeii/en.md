## legend

In the year 79, Pompeii was a busy Roman town on the Bay of Naples. Carts rattled over its stone streets, fountains splashed at every corner, and each morning the bakers slid round loaves out of their brick ovens. Above the town rose Vesuvius, green with vineyards almost to the top. Nobody alive remembered it as a mountain of fire. A great earthquake had struck some years before, and builders were still mending the damage, but in Campania the ground often trembled, and people paid it little mind.

Across the bay, at Misenum, a young man of seventeen named Pliny was staying with his uncle, Pliny the Elder, commander of the Roman fleet. Early in the afternoon his mother pointed out a cloud of strange size and shape rising over the mountain. It looked, he wrote years later, like an umbrella pine: a tall trunk shooting straight up, then spreading out into branches, sometimes white, sometimes dark and dirty with earth and ash.

His uncle launched the warships to rescue people along the coast and sailed straight toward the danger. Ash fell on the decks, hotter and thicker the closer they came, then pumice and blackened stones. At Stabiae he stayed the night with a friend. By morning the courtyard was filling with stones, and the household tied pillows to their heads with cloths and walked out into a darkness blacker than any night. On the shore, choked by the fumes, the old admiral fell and never got up.

In Pompeii, pumice fell hour after hour, piling up in the streets until roofs cracked under its weight. Most people fled, though not all. Through the night and into a morning as dark as night, the great column collapsed, and glowing clouds of scorching gas and ash raced down the mountain faster than anyone could run. They rolled over the town and buried it deep. Across the bay, young Pliny and his mother fled through a darkness, he wrote, like a closed room with the lamp put out.

Pompeii vanished from the map. Grass and vines grew over the ash, and the farmers called the hill la Civita, the city, without knowing what lay beneath. Seventeen centuries passed. Then, in 1748, diggers broke through, and out of the ash came streets and temples, the forum, walls still glowing with red frescoes, and loaves of bread still in the oven: a whole town stopped on a single day.

## beats

- forum: On a bright morning in Pompeii, bakers pull bread from the oven, a cart rolls past and the fountain splashes.
- tremor: The ground shakes, amphorae topple and birds burst from the trees, one more tremor that nobody fears.
- column: At noon Vesuvius blasts a column of ash that spreads across the sky like Pliny's umbrella pine.
- pumice: Pumice and ash rain down, roofs sag, and townsfolk run toward the sea with pillows on their heads.
- surge: At night a glowing cloud of burning ash races down the mountain and rolls over the empty town.
- buried: Pompeii lies under grey ash while lightning flickers in the black cloud, as Pliny saw from across the bay.
- centuries: Time rushes by: grass and vines cover the ash, the stars wheel and a village grows on top.
- dig: Diggers uncover streets and columns, red frescoes glow again, and the town returns to its bright morning.
