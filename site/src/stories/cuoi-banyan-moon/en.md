## legend

Once upon a time, in a village at the edge of the forest, there lived a woodcutter named Cuội. One day, deep in the woods, a tiger cub sprang at him, and he struck it down with his axe. Just then the mother tiger came roaring back, and Cuội dropped everything and scrambled up a tall tree to hide.

From the branches he saw something strange. The tigress went to a banyan tree nearby, chewed a few of its leaves and pressed them into her cub's mouth. Soon the cub stirred and came back to life.

When the tigers had gone, Cuội climbed down, dug up that banyan tree, roots and all, and carried it home. He planted it beside his house and used its leaves to heal the sick. He saved many lives, and once he brought a rich man's daughter back from death, and the rich man gave her to him as his wife.

One day his wife slipped and cracked her head. Cuội pressed the magic leaves on the wound again and again, but she did not wake. So he shaped a new brain for her out of clay, and only then did the leaves bring her back. But from that day on she forgot everything.

Cuội warned her many times: "Water the tree only with clean water. If you pour dirty water on it, it will fly away to the sky." But she forgot. One day she poured dirty water at the foot of the tree. At once its roots pulled free of the soil, and the banyan began to float up into the air.

Cuội was just coming home. He ran, leapt and caught hold of the roots to drag the tree back down. But the tree was stronger. It rose and rose, carrying Cuội with it, all the way up to the moon.

And there he stays. When the moon is full, you can see the dark shape of a great banyan tree on it, with a small figure sitting at its foot. Every year on the fifteenth night of the eighth lunar month, at the Mid-Autumn Festival, children carry star-shaped lanterns through the streets, sing songs about Cuội and look up to find him, sitting under his banyan far from home.
