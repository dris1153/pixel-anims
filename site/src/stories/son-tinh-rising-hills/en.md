## legend

The eighteenth Hùng king had a daughter named Mị Nương, as lovely as a flower and gentle by nature. The king loved her dearly and wanted to find her a worthy husband. One day two suitors came to his court.

The first was Sơn Tinh, the lord of Tản Viên mountain. When he waved his hand toward the east, fields and sandbanks rose from the earth. When he waved it toward the west, whole ranges of hills sprang up. The second was Thủy Tinh, the lord of the waters. When he called the wind, the wind came. When he called the rain, the rain poured down.

Both were equal in power, and the king could not choose between them. So he said: "Tomorrow, whoever first brings the wedding gifts will take my daughter home: a hundred trays of sticky rice, a hundred bánh chưng cakes, an elephant with nine tusks, a rooster with nine spurs and a horse with nine red manes, a pair of each."

At first light Sơn Tinh arrived with every gift and carried Mị Nương away to his mountain. Thủy Tinh came too late. In his fury he led his armies of water after Sơn Tinh to take the princess back. He called up rain and wind, thunder shook the sky, and the rivers swelled until the fields and villages were drowned and the citadel of Phong Châu seemed to float on the flood.

But Sơn Tinh did not give way. With his magic he lifted up the hills and moved whole mountains, and he built walls to hold back the water. However high the water rose, the hills rose just as high. The battle raged for months, until Thủy Tinh was worn out and had to pull his waters back.

Thủy Tinh never forgot. Every year, when the rains come, he sends his storms and floods against the mountain lord again, and every year he loses and draws back. That is how the people of old explained the floods of each rainy season, and the hills that always hold.

## beats

- rain: Sơn Tinh stands on his hilltop as Thủy Tinh's storm drives rain across the misty hills.
- flood: The flood water climbs from the valleys below, swallowing the lower slopes.
- raise: He raises both arms and the hills climb step by step, rising as high as the water rises.
- drain: Beaten again, the flood drains back down the valleys, as Thủy Tinh pulls his waters back.
- clear: The rain stops and the sky clears over the hills, until next year's storm.
