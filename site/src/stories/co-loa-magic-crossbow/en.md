## legend

When King An Dương Vương ruled the land of Âu Lạc, he set out to build a citadel. But however much his people raised by day, the walls came crashing down by night. Old tellers say evil spirits were at work, among them a white rooster that had lived for a thousand years. The king fasted and prayed to all the gods for help.

One morning a Golden Turtle came swimming up the river from the east. It spoke in a human voice and called itself the envoy of the river god. With its help the walls stood firm, and in half a month the citadel was done: rings of ramparts coiling like a snail's shell. So it was called Loa Thành, the Snail Citadel of Cổ Loa.

When the turtle took its leave, the king asked, "If enemies come, what shall I fight them with?" The turtle pulled off one of its claws. "Make this the trigger of a crossbow and aim it at the enemy, and you will have nothing to fear." The king's general Cao Lỗ built the crossbow, and no army could stand against it.

Soon Triệu Đà marched south. One shot of the magic crossbow sent his army running. Beaten, Triệu Đà asked for peace and a marriage: his son Trọng Thủy to the king's daughter, Mỵ Châu. The king agreed. Trọng Thủy coaxed his young wife into showing him the magic crossbow, and in secret he swapped the claw for a false one. Then he left to visit his father. Mỵ Châu told him, "I wear a cloak of goose feathers. If we are ever parted, I will scatter them wherever I go, so you can find me."

Trọng Thủy carried the claw home, and Triệu Đà attacked at once. The king found the magic claw gone. He fled south on horseback with Mỵ Châu behind him, and her feathers marked the road for Trọng Thủy to follow.

At the sea there was no boat. The king cried out for the Golden Turtle, and it rose from the waves: "The enemy sits behind you!" The king drew his sword. Before it fell, Mỵ Châu prayed that if she had betrayed her father she would turn to dust, but if she had been deceived she would turn to pearls. Then the king took a seven-inch rhinoceros horn, and the turtle parted the sea and led him down beneath the waves.

Mỵ Châu's blood flowed into the sea, and the oysters that drank it grew pearls. Trọng Thủy, mad with grief, threw himself into a well at Cổ Loa. People say pearls from that sea shine brightest when washed in water from that well.

## beats

- build: By day the spiral ramparts of Cổ Loa rise ring by ring as An Dương Vương watches from the palace steps.
- haunt: At night the ghostly white rooster Bạch Kê swoops over the walls and the fresh earth crumbles away.
- kim quy: The Golden Turtle rises from the river, the rooster bursts into feathers, and the king fits its claw to his crossbow.
- volley: Triệu Đà's soldiers march in, and one shot of the magic crossbow rains hundreds of golden arrows on them.
- swap: At night Trọng Thủy lifts the glowing claw, leaves a dull copy, and the crossbow goes dark.
- fall: The army returns with torches, the king's arrows tumble limp into the river, and the citadel burns.
- flight: The king gallops toward the sea with Mỵ Châu behind him, her goose-feather cloak shedding a trail of feathers.
- pearls: At the shore the Golden Turtle names the enemy, the sword flashes, and Mỵ Châu's light falls into the oysters as pearls.
- part: Holding the rhinoceros horn, the king follows the Golden Turtle into the sea as the water parts in two walls.
- return: The sea grows calm, dawn breaks and the ramparts of Cổ Loa rise again, ready for the tale to begin anew.
