origin: Japan · Kojiki and Nihon Shoki, 8th century

## legend

Susanoo, god of storms and brother of the sun goddess Amaterasu, was so wild that the gods drove him out of the High Plain of Heaven. He came down to earth in the land of Izumo, beside the river Hi. As he walked along the bank, he saw chopsticks floating down the current. "Someone must live upstream," he thought, and he followed the river.

Soon he found an old man and an old woman weeping, with a young girl between them. The old man was Ashinazuchi, his wife was Tenazuchi, and the girl was their daughter Kushinada. "Once we had eight daughters," the old man said. "But every year the serpent Yamata no Orochi comes and eats one. Now it is time for it to come again, and she is the last."

"What does it look like?" asked Susanoo. "Its eyes are red as winter cherries," said the old man. "It has one body with eight heads and eight tails. Moss and cypress and cedar grow on its back. It stretches across eight valleys and eight hills, and its belly is always red and running with blood."

Susanoo told them he was Amaterasu's brother and asked for Kushinada as his wife, and they gladly agreed. At once he turned her into a comb and slid it into his hair, where no serpent could reach her. Then he told the old couple to brew strong sake, refined eight times over, and to build a fence with eight gates. At each gate they set a platform, and on each platform a vat of sake.

The serpent came, just as the old man had said. It dipped one head into each vat and drank until every drop was gone. Then it lay down, drunk, and fell asleep. Susanoo drew his long sword and cut the monster to pieces, and the river Hi ran red.

When he cut into the middle tail, the edge of his sword chipped. Wondering, he split the tail open and found a great sword inside. It seemed so marvellous that he sent it up to Amaterasu. It came to be called Kusanagi, and it is one of the three sacred treasures of Japan.

Then Susanoo looked for a place to build a palace, and he came to Suga. "Here my heart feels fresh and clear," he said, and he built his palace there. As clouds rose above it, he sang what is said to be the first poem of Japan: "Eightfold clouds rise over Izumo, making an eightfold fence, to hide my wife within an eightfold fence, oh, that eightfold fence!"

## beats

- river: At dusk the storm god Susanoo strides down to the river Hi and points at chopsticks floating downstream.
- elders: The old couple weep at the hut beside Kushinada as the ghosts of her seven lost sisters rise and fade.
- comb: In a flash of light Kushinada becomes a comb, Susanoo slides it into his hair, and the elders go inside.
- sake: Susanoo builds the fence of eight red gates with a vat of strong sake in each, and night falls.
- orochi: The serpent comes over the hills, its belly glowing like lava and eight heads rising with eyes like winter cherries.
- drink: Each head plunges into a vat and gulps, then droops and falls asleep, snoring.
- strike: Susanoo runs along the fence cutting through the necks one after another, each in a burst of sparks.
- tail: His blade chips on the middle tail, and he splits it open and raises the shining sword Kusanagi.
- clouds: The serpent melts into mist, eightfold clouds boil up over Izumo at dawn, and Kushinada steps free from the comb.
- palace: The palace at Suga rises with red pillars, the two walk inside, and dusk turns it back into the old hut.
