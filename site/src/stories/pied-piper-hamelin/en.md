## legend

In the year 1284, the old town of Hamelin on the river Weser was overrun with rats. They gnawed the grain and raided the kitchens, fought the dogs and killed the cats, and no trap or poison could stop them. The townsfolk marched on the town hall and shouted at the mayor to do something, or else.

One day a tall stranger walked into town, in a long coat of many colours, half red and half yellow, with a pipe hanging at his side. He told the mayor and council that he could rid Hamelin of every rat. "Will you give me a thousand guilders?" he asked. "A thousand? Fifty thousand!" cried the mayor, and promised gladly.

The stranger stepped into the street, put the pipe to his lips and began to play. At the first notes the rats came tumbling out of every house, great rats and small rats, brown, black and grey, and followed him down the street in a squeaking river. He walked to the Weser, and they plunged in after him and were drowned, every one.

But now that the rats were gone, a thousand guilders seemed far too much for a bit of piping. The mayor laughed and offered fifty. The piper's face darkened. Those who cheated him, he warned, might hear him play another kind of tune.

He stepped into the street once more and played a new melody, sweet and strange. Out of every house ran the children, boys and girls, clapping and dancing after him, while the mayor and council stood still as blocks of wood, unable to move or call them back. He led them out of the town toward the Koppen hill, and a door opened in its side. The piper went in, the children after him, and the door shut fast.

Only one child was left behind: a little lame boy who could not keep up. Ever after he grieved, for the piper had promised them a wonderful land, and the door had closed just as he reached it. A hundred and thirty children, the old account says, were lost that day.

Hamelin never forgot. Around 1300 the story was set in a stained-glass window in the market church, and to this day, in the street where the children walked, no one may play music or beat a drum.

## beats

- rats: Rats pour through the golden afternoon street, out of barrels and doors, while a baker chases them with his broom.
- piper: A tall stranger in red and yellow patches walks in, and the fat mayor shakes his hand, holding up a bag of gold.
- tune: He plays his pipe, notes float out, and the rats stream from every door in a long line behind him.
- river: He leads them into the Weser, and the current sweeps them all away downstream.
- refuse: The mayor laughs and holds out an empty purse, and the Piper's face darkens as dusk falls.
- children: A new, glowing tune draws the children skipping out of the houses and up the street after him.
- mountain: A doorway of light opens in the Koppen hill, takes in the Piper and the children, and shuts, leaving the lame boy outside.
- return: At a cold dawn the church window glows with the story, the lame boy limps home and the rats creep back into the street.
