---
origin: India · Hindu myth, told in the Bhagavata Purana, the Vishnu Purana and the Mahabharata
---

## legend

Long ago the devas, the gods of heaven, lost their strength and their good fortune, and their rivals the asuras grew mighty. The devas went to Vishnu for help. "Churn the Ocean of Milk," he told them, "and from its depths will rise amrita, the nectar of immortality. But you cannot do it alone. Make peace with the asuras and churn together."

So the old enemies made a truce. They set Mount Mandara in the sea as a churning rod and wrapped Vasuki, king of serpents, around it as the rope. The asuras demanded the head, so the devas took the tail, and as the two sides pulled back and forth, the serpent's fiery breath scorched the asuras. But the mountain began to sink into the sea, until Vishnu became Kurma, a giant tortoise, and held it up on his back.

The first thing to rise was not nectar but Halahala, a poison so deadly that its fumes began to choke the world. Gods and demons fled. Only Shiva came forward. He gathered the poison and drank it, and his wife Parvati pressed his throat so it would go no further. There it stayed, turning his throat blue, and ever since he has been called Neelakantha, the Blue-Throated One.

Then treasures rose one by one: the wish-granting cow, a white horse with seven heads, Airavata the white elephant, the moon, which Shiva took to wear in his hair, and Lakshmi, goddess of fortune, standing on a lotus, who chose Vishnu as her husband. Last came Dhanvantari, physician of the gods, holding the pot of amrita.

The asuras snatched it at once. But Vishnu appeared as Mohini, an enchantress so lovely that they forgot their quarrel and let her share out the nectar, and she poured it for the devas. One asura, Svarbhanu, disguised himself as a god and sat in their line between the sun and the moon. He had just sipped when the sun and moon recognised him. Vishnu hurled his discus, the Sudarshana, and cut off his head before the nectar could pass his throat.

The head had tasted amrita, so it could never die. It became Rahu, and he still chases the sun and the moon for revenge. When he catches one and swallows it, the sky darkens in an eclipse. But Rahu has no body, and soon the light slips out again through his severed neck.

## beats

- churn: Devas and asuras heave on Vasuki like a tug of war, and the serpent's fiery breath scorches the asura lord.
- kurma: The mountain sinks into a whirlpool until the great tortoise Kurma rises beneath it and lifts it back up.
- halahala: Violet-black poison bursts from the churn, the milk stains violet and both leaders drop the serpent in terror.
- neelakantha: Shiva draws the whole poison cloud into his mouth, his throat blazes blue and the milk turns white again.
- treasures: Airavata, the white four-tusked elephant, bursts out trumpeting, then the crescent moon lifts dripping from the milk.
- lakshmi: A giant pink lotus opens and Lakshmi rises in red and gold, gold coins spilling from her hand.
- amrita: Dhanvantari lifts the pot of nectar, the asura lord snatches it, and Mohini dances in and charms it away.
- rahu: A disguised asura sips the nectar, the sun and moon expose him, and Mohini's discus cuts off his head.
- eclipse: Rahu's head swallows the moon and the sky turns copper, until the moon slips out through his severed neck.
- return: The moon sinks back into the milk, the tortoise dives, and both sides take up the serpent to churn again.
