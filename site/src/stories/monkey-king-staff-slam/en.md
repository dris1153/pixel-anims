## legend

On Flower Fruit Mountain, behind the waterfall called the Water Curtain, lived the Monkey King, Sun Wukong, who was born from a stone. He had travelled far to learn the secret arts of the immortals: seventy-two transformations, and a cloud somersault that carried him a hundred and eight thousand li in one leap. Now he was home again, king of all the monkeys, and he wanted a weapon worthy of his skill.

He tried swords and spears, but none felt right in his hand. Then the old monkeys said, "Your Majesty, the water under our iron bridge flows all the way to the Dragon Palace of the East Sea. Why not ask the Dragon King for a weapon?"

So Wukong parted the waters with a spell and walked down to the Crystal Palace. The Dragon King, Ao Guang, did not dare refuse such a guest. His servants carried out a great sword, then a nine-pronged fork, then a painted halberd weighing seven thousand two hundred pounds. Wukong swung each one and tossed it aside. "Too light!"

Then the Dragon Queen whispered to her husband. In the sea treasury stood an iron pillar that the Great Yu had once used to measure the depth of the rivers and seas when he tamed the flood. For days it had been glowing with a strange light, as if it were waiting for someone.

Wukong went to see. The pillar was as thick as a barrel and more than twenty feet tall. He stroked it and said, "A little thinner and shorter would be better," and at once it shrank. "A little more," he said, and it shrank again. When he lifted it out, it was a staff of black iron with a golden band at each end, and on it were written the words: Ruyi Jingu Bang, the As-You-Will Gold-Banded Staff, thirteen thousand five hundred pounds.

Delighted, Wukong whirled it around the palace until the Dragon King trembled, and then he carried it home.

Back on Flower Fruit Mountain he shrank it to the size of an embroidery needle and tucked it into his ear, and when his monkeys begged to see it again, he took it out. "Grow!" he cried, and it swelled as he swelled with it, until the staff in his hand reached up to the highest heaven and down to the deepest hell, and every beast on the mountain shook with fear. Then he made himself small again, shrank the staff back into a needle, and hid it in his ear, where it waits to this day.

## beats

- idle: Sun Wukong stands on a small cloud before the Water Curtain, one hand shading his eyes, the other on his hip.
- grow: He holds out the tiny needle and it swells into the gold-banded staff, just as it grew for his monkeys.
- twirl: He whirls the staff twice around before his chest, then cocks it high behind his head.
- slam: Both hands drive the staff down across the valley, and it strikes the ground in a ring of dust.
- shrink: The staff shrinks back into a needle, and he tucks it away behind his ear.
- settle: He settles on his cloud and shades his eyes again, keeping watch over Flower Fruit Mountain.
