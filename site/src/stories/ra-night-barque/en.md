origin: Egypt · The Amduat and the Book of Gates, painted in New Kingdom royal tombs

## legend

Every day, the ancient Egyptians said, the sun god Ra sails across the sky in a golden boat, under the arching body of Nut, the sky goddess. But when the sun sinks in the west, his journey is only half done. Ra boards his night barque and sails down into the Duat, the underworld, along a dark river that runs through the twelve hours of the night. Gods sail with him to protect him, among them Isis, mistress of magic, falcon-headed Horus, and mighty Set, who stands at the prow.

Each hour of the night is closed by a great gate, guarded by serpents and fire-spitting cobras. Ra speaks the words of power, and the gates swing open. As his light passes, the souls of the dead, the ba birds with human heads, wake and cry out for joy. In the Field of Reeds the blessed dead harvest grain taller than a man. When the barque sails on, they sink back into the dark to wait for the next night.

At midnight, in the deepest hour, Ra comes to Osiris, lord of the dead, lying still in the dark. The two gods join as one, the sun's soul with Osiris's body, and from this union the sun draws new life.

But in the seventh hour lies Apep, the vast serpent of chaos and eternal enemy of the sun, who would swallow the world back into darkness if he could. Apep drinks the river dry, and the sun boat runs aground on his sandbank. Then Isis chants her strongest spells, other gods bind the serpent's head and tail, and knives are driven into his coils. In many pictures Set stands at the prow and spears him again and again. Apep is beaten back, but never killed. He will be waiting again tomorrow night.

In the last hour, the gods haul the barque into the tail of an enormous serpent and all the way through its body. What goes in old comes out young. From the serpent's mouth Ra emerges as Khepri, the scarab beetle, who rolls the new sun up over the eastern horizon just as a scarab rolls its ball of earth. Baboons raise their arms and chatter to greet him. And so Ra climbs into the day sky once more, to sail again toward the west.

## beats

- sunset: The golden barque sinks out of the red and violet western sky onto the river of the underworld.
- gate: It passes the great pylon gate, where a rearing cobra spits an arc of fire across the water.
- souls: In the dark river, golden ba birds with human heads flutter up to greet the sun's light.
- reeds: The Field of Reeds glows gold in the darkness while the blessed dead harvest its grain.
- apep: Apep heaves his coils from the river and drinks it dry, and the barque runs aground on a sandbank.
- spear: Set drives his spear into Apep's rearing head again and again while Isis raises bands of binding light.
- bound: Knives stand in the coils, Apep sinks away, the river flows back and the barque floats free.
- osiris: In the deepest hour green-faced Osiris lies on his bier, and his light and Ra's merge in a burst of gold.
- tunnel: The barque slides into the tail of a great golden serpent, its glow travelling through the body.
- khepri: It bursts from the serpent's mouth, and the scarab Khepri rolls a new red sun up out of the east.
- sunrise: Baboons on the pyramid raise their arms to greet the dawn as the Nile valley floods with light.
- day: The barque climbs into the blue day sky and sails back west under Nut's arch, ready to sink again.
