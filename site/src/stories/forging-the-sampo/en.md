## legend

Far in the North lies Pohjola, a land of long nights and cold seas, ruled by Louhi, the gap-toothed Mistress of the North. When old Väinämöinen, the singer, drifted to her shore half-drowned, Louhi nursed him and asked a favour: could he forge her a Sampo, a mill of plenty? To whoever made one she would give her lovely daughter. Väinämöinen could not, but he knew who could: Ilmarinen, the eternal smith, who had hammered out the very vault of the sky.

Ilmarinen had no wish to go north. So Väinämöinen sang up a tall spruce with the moon shining in its crown and the Great Bear in its branches, and when the smith climbed up to fetch them, Väinämöinen sang up a storm wind that carried him off through the clouds, all the way to Pohjola.

He took the things Louhi had named: the tip of a swan's feather, the milk of a barren cow, a single grain of barley and the wool of a summer ewe. He laid them in the fire and set his thralls to work the bellows. First a golden crossbow rose from the flames, beautiful but cruel: it would want a head every day, and two on good days. He broke it and threw it back. Next came a red boat that would sail off to war for no reason, and he broke that too. A golden-horned heifer and a golden plough came after, both bad, and he broke them as well.

Then Ilmarinen sent the thralls away and called on the winds. The east wind blew and the west, the south wind roared and the north wind howled. For three days they worked the bellows, until fire leapt from the windows and sparks flew up to the clouds. When the smith bent over the forge, he saw the Sampo rising, with its lid of many colours. He hammered it into shape, with a flour mill on one side, a salt mill on another and a money mill on the third. At once it began to grind: a binful for eating, a binful for selling and a binful for feasting at home.

Louhi was overjoyed. She carried the Sampo into the stony hill of Pohjola, into the belly of the copper mountain, and shut it behind nine locks, and there it put down roots nine fathoms deep. But her daughter would not go with the smith, and Ilmarinen sailed home alone. Years later the heroes of Kaleva sailed north to take the Sampo, and in the fight at sea it shattered. The pieces that washed ashore, they say, still bring luck to the fields of Finland.

## beats

- forge: Under the long arctic night Ilmarinen pumps the bellows in his log smithy, and sparks shoot up toward the stars.
- bow: A golden crossbow leaps from the fire, and since it would want blood every day, he snaps it and throws it back.
- boat: A red boat bursts out of the flames, eager for war, so the smith breaks it too.
- winds: The four winds blow the bellows by themselves, the forge roars white-hot and the aurora flares green and violet.
- sampo: The Sampo rises from the flames, the magic mill whose many-coloured lid spins by itself.
- grind: It grinds a bin of flour, a bin of salt and a bin of gold coins that spill over onto the snow.
- lock: Louhi, the grey Mistress of the North, carries the Sampo into the copper mountain behind nine locks, and roots grow over the door.
- night: The forge cools to embers, snow falls and the aurora dims, until the fire is lit again.
