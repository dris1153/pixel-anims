## legend

Oisín was the son of Fionn mac Cumhaill, leader of the Fianna, the band of warriors who hunted and fought across the green hills of Ireland. Of all of them, Oisín was the finest poet, and his songs could make the hardest warrior weep.

One morning, as the Fianna hunted along the shore, a white horse came galloping over the waves as if the sea were solid ground. On its back rode a woman with hair of gold, lovelier than anyone they had ever seen. She was Niamh of the Golden Hair, daughter of the king of Tír na nÓg, the Land of Youth, and she had come for Oisín. She held out her hand, and he climbed up behind her without a second thought.

The horse, Embarr, carried them away across the sea. As they rode, strange visions ran beside them on the water: a hornless deer chased by a white hound with one red ear, a lady fleeing on horseback from a rider with a golden apple.

In Tír na nÓg no one grows old and no one is ever sick. It is always summer there; the apple trees blossom and bear fruit at the same time, and the feasting never ends. Oisín married Niamh and was happy. But after three years he began to miss his father and his friends, and he asked to go home, just for a visit.

Niamh let him take Embarr, and gave him one warning: "Do not get down from the horse. If your foot touches the soil of Ireland, you can never come back to me."

Oisín rode home and found everything changed. The great fort of the Fianna was a grassy ruin, and the people were small and weak, and no one had seen Fionn for three hundred years, for that is how long three years in the Land of Youth had been. Riding on, he came upon men struggling to lift a stone. Oisín leaned down from the saddle to help, and lifted it with one hand. But the girth snapped, and he fell to the ground.

At once three hundred years fell on him. Oisín was a withered old man with a long white beard, and Embarr, riderless, turned and galloped back into the sea, toward the island he would never see again.

## beats

- hunt: The Fianna hunt along the clifftop with their hounds, Oisín with his harp on his back.
- niamh: Niamh of the Golden Hair rides out of the sea on the white horse Embarr and holds out her hand.
- ride: Oisín leaps up behind her and they gallop across the waves, ghostly deer and hound beside them.
- youth: In Tír na nÓg it is endless summer; blossoms fall and Oisín plays his harp.
- years: The seasons whirl past: three hundred years in the blink of an eye.
- home: Oisín rides home alone and finds the fort in ruins and small farmers struggling with a stone.
- fall: He leans down to help, the girth snaps, and he turns into an old man.
- return: The old man fades, Embarr gallops back toward Niamh's island, and the Fianna's horns sound again.
