## legend

Before there was a sky or an earth, everything was chaos: dark, swirling and shapeless, like the inside of a hen's egg. In that egg the giant Pangu was born, and there he lay curled up in the dark for eighteen thousand years.

Then heaven and earth broke open. The clear, light part of the chaos, the yang, drifted up and became the sky. The heavy, murky part, the yin, sank down and became the earth. Later folk tellings put a great axe in Pangu's hands: he wakes, swings it once, and the egg bursts apart.

Pangu stood between the two so they could not close again. Each day the sky rose one zhang, ten Chinese feet, the earth grew one zhang thicker, and Pangu grew one zhang taller to keep them apart. So it went for eighteen thousand more years, until the sky was as high as it could go, the earth as deep as it could go, and Pangu as tall as he could grow. By then heaven stood ninety thousand li above the earth. So wrote the scholar Xu Zheng in the third century, in his Sanwu Liji (三五历纪).

Another old book, the Wuyun Linianji (五运历年纪), tells how it ended. His work done, Pangu lay down, and as he died his body became the world. His breath became the wind and the clouds, and his voice the thunder. His left eye became the sun and his right eye the moon. His limbs and body became the four poles of the world and the five great peaks. His blood ran as rivers, his sinews became roads and his flesh became fields. His hair and beard rose as the stars. His skin and its fine hair grew into grass and trees. His teeth and bones turned to metal and stone, his marrow to pearls and jade, and his sweat fell as rain.

A later book of wonders, the Shuyi Ji (述异记), even names the mountains. His head became Mount Tai in the east and his belly Mount Song in the center. His left arm became Mount Heng in the south, his right arm the other Mount Heng in the north, and his feet Mount Hua in the west.

So the giant who pushed heaven and earth apart never really left. He is still there in the peaks and the rivers, in the wind and the rain, in the sun by day and the stars by night.

## beats

- egg: In the black void of chaos the vast egg turns slowly, a faint glow beating inside it like a heart.
- sleep: The glow brightens to show Pangu curled up asleep inside, while dust and embers drift through eighteen thousand years.
- wake: Pangu's eyes open as two sparks, he stretches, and bright cracks race across the shell.
- axe: He swings the great axe of the later folk tales, and the egg bursts in white light and flying shards.
- split: The clear, light yang drifts up as the sky and the murky yin sinks as the earth, swirling apart.
- hold: Bearded and horned, Pangu stands between them so they cannot close again, palms against the sky, feet braced on the earth.
- grow: Days and nights flicker past ever faster as Pangu grows a zhang a day, pushing the sky higher and the earth lower.
- weary: The sky holds by itself at last; Pangu lowers his arms, lets the axe fall and sinks to one knee.
- fall: He lies down along the horizon, head to the east and feet to the west, and breathes out one last great breath.
- breath: His breath rolls out as wind and billowing clouds, and his voice cracks across them as thunder and lightning.
- eyes: His left eye rises into the sky as a blazing sun and his right eye as a silver moon.
- peaks: His body turns to stone: head, belly and feet rise as the sacred peaks, and his arms stretch into long ridges.
- rivers: His blood winds out as rivers between the peaks to a shining sea, and his sweat falls as rain.
- stars: Night falls, and his hair and beard lift into the sky and scatter as countless stars around the moon.
- bloom: At first dawn his skin's fine hair sprouts as grass, trees and flowers, and his bones and marrow glint as gold and jade.
- return: At dusk the whole world spirals back into one point and closes into the egg around the sleeping giant, so the tale can begin again.
